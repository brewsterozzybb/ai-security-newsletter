## 1. Headliners

### 99.9% of Fixable AI Vulnerabilities Remain Unpatched — Orca Security Report

Orca Security published its **2026 State of AI Security Report** on July 13, and the headline statistic is damning: **99.9% of AI vulnerability alerts with an available fix remain unpatched.** The finding is based on analysis of AI workloads across major cloud providers and represents the largest systematic audit of AI infrastructure security posture to date.

The full picture is worse than the headline. Orca found that 81.2% of companies running AI packages have at least one known vulnerability, and 74.1% have at least one critical CVE. AI packages inherit vulnerabilities disclosed over the past five years — including CVEs published during the last 12 months — exposing production environments to both old and new threats simultaneously. A vulnerable library embedded in a dependency graph often outlives the patch cycle, and AI workloads inherit the same problem despite release cycles that assume dependencies remain up to date.

**Agent frameworks in production, security in the parking lot.** Fifty-six percent of AI adopters have deployed agent frameworks into production, and 51.5% use AI to build custom applications. Every production agent represents a new non-human identity with its own permissions, memory, and potential blast radius. Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines allow LLMs to access internal documents, customer data, and proprietary knowledge at query time — and 64% of AI adopters have deployed vector databases, operating an average of 3.78 per organization.

**The encryption gap.** Between 87% and 98% of organizations across the three major cloud providers have not configured customer-managed encryption keys for their AI services. Organizations manage complex AI ecosystems connected to enterprise data, cloud services, identities, and production workflows — with no unified visibility.

**Why it matters:** The 99.9% figure is the most important AI security statistic of 2026 so far. It means that for every thousand fixable vulnerabilities in AI infrastructure, exactly one gets patched. The window for exploitation is not a window — it is the default state. For security teams, the implication is straightforward: treat every AI package in your dependency graph as actively vulnerable until proven otherwise, and verify patching through automated scans rather than trust in release cycles.

**Source:** [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/07/13/ai-infrastructure-security-risks-report/) | [Orca Security](https://orca.security/)

## 2. Breaking News

### CISA Adds Two CVSS 10.0 Zero-Days to KEV — iCagenda and Balbooa Joomla Extensions Actively Exploited

CISA added two vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on July 10, both carrying the maximum CVSS severity score of 10.0. Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies have until **July 13, 2026** — today — to apply patches.

**CVE-2026-48939** affects the iCagenda Joomla extension through its "Submit an Event" feature, enabling attackers to upload arbitrary PHP files and execute malicious code. mySites.guru, which first detected the exploitation, reported that automated scanners identifying themselves as "icagenda-batch/1.0" have been exploiting the flaw since June 15, 2026. The attack chain: an automated scanner grabs a token, posts a malicious upload to the submit endpoint, then fetches the planted shell at the exact path the component writes attachments to.

**CVE-2026-56291** affects Balbooa Forms up to version 2.4.0. The frontend attachment upload accepted a file from any anonymous visitor — no login, no CSRF token, no check on the file type. An attacker could upload a PHP file into a public folder and then execute it.

**The AI acceleration angle.** The Australian Cyber Security Centre (ACSC) separately warned of a global campaign targeting vulnerable CMS platforms and plugins, explicitly noting that "advances in AI are accelerating the speed and scale of cyber operations, reducing the time between vulnerability disclosure and exploitation." The ACSC advisory covers 12 additional CVEs across CMS platforms, linking the campaign to the same threat actors exploiting the iCagenda and Balbooa flaws.

**Why it matters:** Two CVSS 10.0 vulnerabilities being actively exploited in automated campaigns — and the federal patch deadline is today. The ACSC's explicit linkage to AI acceleration is the first time a major national cybersecurity agency has formally attributed exploitation speed increases to AI. This is not a theoretical concern; it is operational reality.

**Sources:** [The Cyber Express](https://thecyberexpress.com/cisa-cve-2026-48939-cve-2026-56291/) | [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/07/10/cisa-adds-two-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) | [mySites.guru](https://mysites.guru/blog/icagenda-zero-day-file-upload-rce/)

### Cisco Transitions to Risk-Based Vulnerability Disclosure, Citing AI-Driven Threat Discovery

Cisco announced on July 11 that it is transitioning to a **risk-based vulnerability disclosure model**, citing the "rapid evolution of AI-driven cyber threat discovery and mitigation" as the primary driver. The change means Cisco will no longer publish all vulnerabilities on a fixed schedule but will prioritize disclosure based on risk assessment — a significant departure from the industry-standard coordinated disclosure model.

The announcement explicitly links the policy change to the acceleration of vulnerability discovery and exploitation driven by AI tools. "Starting in July 2026, Cisco will further evolve to a risk-based vulnerability disclosure model to address the rapid evolution of AI-driven cyber threat discovery and mitigation," the company's advisory page states.

**Why it matters:** This is the first major networking vendor to formally change its disclosure policy in direct response to AI-driven threat acceleration. The shift has implications for every organization that relies on Cisco infrastructure: if not all vulnerabilities are disclosed on a fixed timeline, how do defenders know what to prioritize? Cisco's move may set a precedent that other hardware and infrastructure vendors follow, changing the disclosure landscape for the entire industry.

**Source:** [Cisco PSIRT](https://sec.cloudapps.cisco.com/security/center/resources/risk-based-disclosure)

## 3. Technical Deep Dives

### ENISA: Frontier AI Has Compressed the Exploitation Gap from Years to Minutes

The European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) published a landmark assessment in July 2026 titled **"ENISA's View on Cybersecurity in the Frontier AI Era,"** and the paper's core finding is a strategic warning for the entire security profession: **frontier AI has compressed the gap between vulnerability disclosure and exploitation from years to minutes, and in some cases below zero.**

The paper documents that attackers are now weaponizing disclosures within 15 minutes of publication. The median time from disclosure to exploitation has collapsed below the threshold that traditional patch cycles can address. The gap between a vulnerability being disclosed and being exploited is now shorter than the time it takes most organizations to assess whether they are affected.

**The five consequences for the Cyber Resilience Act.** The ENISA paper identifies five specific implications for manufacturers under the EU's Cyber Resilience Act (CRA):
1. Vulnerability management must shift from periodic to continuous
2. Incident response timelines must be measured in minutes, not days
3. Security-by-design requirements must account for AI-driven attack automation
4. Patching SLAs must be compressed to match the new exploitation timeline
5. Threat intelligence sharing must become real-time and automated

**Why it matters:** ENISA's formal assessment is the first time a major cybersecurity regulatory body has quantified the exploitation acceleration in concrete terms. The 15-minute weaponization figure is a benchmark that every security team should use to evaluate their own response capabilities. If your organization's vulnerability management process takes longer than 15 minutes to produce a risk assessment, you are operating on a timeline that attackers have already outpaced.

**Sources:** [ENISA](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/enisas-view-on-cybersecurity-in-the-frontier-ai-era) | [CRA Evidence](https://craevidence.com/blog/enisa-frontier-ai-cybersecurity-cra-manufacturers) | [Diamatix](https://diamatix.com/enisa-frontier-ai-vulnerability-management-soc-response/)

### MCP Server Security Audit: 4,982 Issues Across 2,259 Public Servers

A systematic analysis of 9,695 public Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers has cataloged **4,982 security issues across 2,259 affected servers**, according to research published in early July. The audit found that popularity, activity levels, and verification badges offered no reliable protection against security flaws.

**Vulnerability categories identified:**
- Arbitrary file access vulnerabilities allowing agents to read sensitive system files
- Command injection flaws enabling remote code execution through crafted tool inputs
- Server-side request forgery (SSRF) allowing agents to probe internal networks
- SQL injection vulnerabilities in servers that connect to databases
- Server-side template injection enabling unauthorized code execution

**The crypto-MCP concentration problem.** In one case, a prolific developer with over 40 crypto-focused MCP servers had 101 security issues across 13 affected repositories, including server-side template injection and prompt injection that could enable unauthorized blockchain transactions. The finding underscores that the MCP ecosystem's security posture is not correlated with the developer's reputation or output volume.

**Why it matters:** MCP is the connective tissue of the agentic AI ecosystem. Every major vendor is standardizing on it. The finding that 23% of public MCP servers have security issues — and that the worst concentration belongs to a single developer with 40+ servers — means the MCP supply chain is carrying a significant embedded risk. For organizations deploying MCP servers, the immediate action is to audit every server in your dependency graph, verify input validation on every tool, and treat every third-party MCP server as an untrusted external endpoint.

**Sources:** [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/security-issues-mcp-servers/) | [CyberPress](https://cyberpress.org/4982-security-issues-expose-2259-public-mcp-servers-to-ai-agent-attacks/)

## 4. Community Pulse

### KittySploit: Open-Source AI-Powered Penetration Testing Framework Ships with 1,150+ Modules

A new open-source penetration testing framework called **KittySploit** was released on July 12, combining a Python and Zig codebase with autonomous AI agents for offensive security operations. The framework ships with over 1,150 modules covering reconnaissance, exploitation, post-exploitation, and reporting.

The release represents the continued democratization of AI-powered offensive security tools. Unlike earlier frameworks that required manual payload selection and execution, KittySploit's AI agents can autonomously select, configure, and run modules based on the target environment. The framework is dual-use by design — it is intended for legitimate penetration testing but the same capabilities could be repurposed by attackers.

**Why it matters:** Open-source AI pentesting frameworks are becoming more sophisticated and accessible. For defenders, the implication is that the barrier to entry for AI-assisted attacks continues to fall. The same tools that security teams use to find vulnerabilities before attackers do are now available to anyone with a GitHub account.

**Source:** [Cyber Security News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/kittysploit-penetration-testing-tool/)

### Destructive Command Guard (dcg): The Community Builds Agent Guardrails

The **Destructive Command Guard (dcg)** hit #2 on GitHub trending on July 12-13, amassing 3,638 stars with 1,290 stars added in a single day. The tool is a Rust-based hook that sits between AI coding agents and the shell, blocking dangerous git and shell commands — the `rm -rf` class of accident — from being executed by agents in under a millisecond.

The tool's emergence is a direct response to the wave of agent-triggered destructive command incidents documented over the past month, including the Mozilla 0DIN DNS TXT record attack, the GhostApproval symlink attack, and the general class of agent-induced data loss. The project's README is explicit about its scope: "It is a seatbelt, not a threat model."

**Why it matters:** The community is building its own defense layer for agentic coding workflows because the tool vendors haven't shipped one yet. The tool's rapid adoption — 1,290 stars in a day — signals that the developer community has recognized the gap between what AI coding agents can do and what the agent runtime is designed to prevent. The seatbelt metaphor is apt: dcg prevents the catastrophic accident that everyone knows is coming, but it does not address the underlying architecture that makes the accident possible.

## 6. Five Things to Take Into the Rest of the Day

1. **Treat every AI package in your dependency graph as actively vulnerable.** Orca's 99.9% unpatched figure means you cannot rely on vendor patch cycles. Run automated vulnerability scanning against your AI infrastructure and verify patching status directly. The default assumption should be that any AI package in production has an unpatched CVE.

2. **Audit your MCP server supply chain.** The 4,982-issue audit shows that 23% of public MCP servers have security flaws, and popularity is not protection. Verify input validation, authentication, and rate limiting on every MCP server your agents connect to. Treat third-party MCP servers as untrusted external endpoints.

3. **Your vulnerability management timeline is now measured in minutes.** ENISA's 15-minute weaponization figure is the new benchmark. If your organization takes longer than 15 minutes to assess a disclosed vulnerability for impact, attackers have already weaponized it. Evaluate whether your incident response process can operate at this speed.

4. **Patch iCagenda and Balbooa Joomla extensions today.** CISA's KEV addition means federal agencies have until July 13 — today — but the same vulnerabilities are being exploited against non-federal targets. The ACSC's global campaign advisory confirms the exploitation is automated and widespread.

5. **Install a destructive command guard for your AI coding agents.** The community-built dcg tool is a stopgap, not a solution, but it is better than nothing. Until agent runtimes ship native protection against destructive shell commands, a Rust hook that blocks `rm -rf` in under a millisecond is the practical defense. The seatbelt saves lives even if it doesn't fix the car.
