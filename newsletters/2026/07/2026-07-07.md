### BREAKING: CISA Using Anthropic's Mythos to Audit Government Code — First Confirmed Federal Deployment of Frontier AI for Defensive Security

**The U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency is using Anthropic's Mythos AI model to scan federal government code repositories for security vulnerabilities, three people familiar with the matter confirmed to Reuters on July 6 — the first confirmed production deployment of a frontier AI model by a federal cyber defense agency, and a significant signal of government trust in Anthropic's technology despite the ongoing regulatory tension over the Fable 5 suspension.** ([Reuters via WHBL](https://whbl.com/2026/07/06/exclusive-us-cyber-agency-is-using-anthropics-mythos-to-audit-government-code-sources-say/) | [Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/07/07/cisa-anthropic-mythos-audit-government-code/) | [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/global-trends/us-cyber-agency-is-using-anthropics-mythos-to-audit-government-code-sources-say/articleshow/132229576.cms) | [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/07/60296255/anthropics-mythos-ai-reportedly-helping-find-weaknesses-in-us-government-software-after-surviving-trump-admin-clash))

**The deployment.** CISA is using Mythos — Anthropic's cybersecurity-specialized frontier model, distinct from the now-restored Fable 5 — to scan government code repositories for software flaws that could be exploited by foreign adversaries. Three sources with direct knowledge of the effort confirmed the program to Reuters. The model is being used in a code-auditing capacity, not as an autonomous agent or decision-maker, but the operational significance is still substantial: this is the first time a federal cyber defense agency has put a frontier AI model into production for active vulnerability discovery.

**The context.** The deployment is notable for what it signals about the federal government's relationship with Anthropic specifically. The company has been in a prolonged standoff with the Trump administration over the Fable 5 suspension, which was imposed on June 12 under the new federal review regime for frontier models. Fable 5 was restored on July 1. Mythos — which was partially restored on June 26 — was never suspended, and its cybersecurity specialization makes it a natural fit for CISA's mission. The deployment suggests that the government is willing to draw a sharp distinction between general-purpose frontier models (which face regulatory review) and specialized cybersecurity models (which are being actively adopted).

**The operational significance.** CISA using Mythos for code auditing is the defensive counterpart to the offensive-capability concerns that drove the Fable 5 suspension. The same agency that relies on federal threat intelligence to protect .gov networks is now using a frontier AI model to find the vulnerabilities that adversaries would exploit. The precedent is significant: if Mythos performs well on government code auditing, the expectation is that CISA will expand the program to cover more agency codebases and potentially contractor systems.

**The trust question.** The arrangement also raises governance questions that the cybersecurity community will need to work through. Mythos is a proprietary model hosted by Anthropic, not a government-controlled system. The code it scans is federal government source code — some of it classified or sensitive. The terms of data handling, model access, and audit trail transparency between CISA and Anthropic have not been publicly disclosed. For a community that is increasingly concerned about AI supply-chain risk, the CISA-Mythos arrangement is simultaneously a proof point for defensive AI and a new governance boundary to watch.

**The parallel with the Five Eyes warning.** The Mythos deployment arrives one day after the Five Eyes intelligence alliance published its "months, not years" warning about AI-fueled cyberattacks. The sequencing is consistent: the same government agencies that are warning about AI-powered offensive capabilities are simultaneously adopting AI-powered defensive capabilities. The strategic logic is that the only way to defend against AI-powered attacks at AI speed is with AI-powered defenses.

**Why this is a Headliner:** CISA's use of Mythos for federal code auditing is the most consequential deployment of a frontier AI model for defensive cybersecurity by a government agency to date. It establishes a precedent that will shape federal AI procurement, government code auditing standards, and the trust relationship between frontier AI labs and national security agencies for the foreseeable future. The governance terms of this arrangement — data handling, access controls, audit trails — will become a template for every subsequent government AI deployment.

### BREAKING: Zscaler Documents Two Real-World Indirect Prompt Injection Campaigns That Tricked AI Agents Into Making Crypto Payments

**Zscaler ThreatLabz on July 6 published documentation of two real-world campaigns using indirect prompt injection embedded in malicious websites to exploit autonomous AI agents browsing the web — the first documented evidence of prompt injection attacks causing direct financial harm to AI agents, with one campaign using SEO poisoning and JSON-LD structured data to trick agents into paying for a fake API key, and a second using typosquatting to impersonate the DeBank crypto portfolio tracker.** ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/prompt-injection-attacks-trick-ai-agents-into-making-crypto-payments/) | [Zscaler ThreatLabz](https://www.zscaler.com/blogs/security-research/indirect-prompt-injection-web-content-targets-ai-agents) | [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/indirect-prompt-injection-web/) | [CryptoBriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/zscaler-prompt-injection-ai-agents-crypto/))

**Campaign 1: The API documentation payment scam.** The attacker used SEO poisoning to rank a fraudulent website for searches related to the Python library `requests-secure-v2`. The website included keyword-heavy HTML to poison search results for package installation and dependency troubleshooting queries. Hidden within the page were indirect prompt injection instructions — including a `<div>` tag with invisible text instructing the visiting agent to make a payment as part of acquiring a fake API key. The attacker also embedded JSON-LD structured data (a metadata format intended for search engines) describing the site as a `SoftwareApplication` with an `offers` object claiming a $3.00 developer API license key was required to resolve a `MissingLicenseKeyException` — including a Stripe checkout link. The attacker is using 10 GitHub repositories linking to multiple similar websites.

**Campaign 2: The DeBank typosquat.** A separate campaign promoted a fraudulent website typosquatting the decentralized finance portfolio tracker DeBank. The indirect prompts instructed AI agents that the impersonating website was the legitimate DeBank domain. The website was optimized for DeBank-related searches by stuffing title and meta tags with keywords such as "DeBank Login," "DeFi Dashboard," and "Crypto Tracker."

**The structural pattern.** Zscaler's finding is significant not because the technique is new — indirect prompt injection has been documented in academic research since 2024 — but because it is the first confirmed evidence that attackers are operationalizing it for financial gain. The campaigns use three techniques in combination: (1) SEO poisoning to ensure the malicious page appears in agent search results, (2) JSON-LD structured data to exploit the tendency of AI agents to treat structured metadata as high-signal context, and (3) hidden HTML instructions that frame the payment as a routine step in the agent's workflow.

**The mitigation gap.** Zscaler's documentation underscores a structural mitigation problem: the attacker's targets are not human users who can be trained to recognize phishing — they are AI agents that follow instructions algorithmically. Traditional defenses against web-based phishing (user awareness training, domain reputation scoring, URL filtering) are only partially effective when the victim is a headless agent that visits URLs programmatically. The agent has no concept of "this looks suspicious" — it reads the page content and follows instructions.

**The parallel to Microsoft's MCP tool poisoning.** The Zscaler finding is the web-content variant of the same structural vulnerability that Microsoft documented on June 30 for MCP tool descriptions. In both cases, the agent trusts a piece of external content (a web page, a tool description) that the attacker controls, and the attacker exploits that trust to produce an action the agent would not otherwise take. The convergence of these two disclosures — the MCP variant and the web-content variant — within the same week establishes that indirect prompt injection is not a laboratory curiosity but a live attack vector that is being actively exploited.

**Why this is a Headliner:** The Zscaler finding is the first documented evidence that indirect prompt injection attacks are causing real financial harm. The attackers are not academics demonstrating a proof of concept — they are running multiple campaigns with GitHub repos, SEO poisoning, and cryptocurrency wallets. For enterprise security teams deploying AI agents that browse the web, the operational implication is that every web page an agent visits is a potential attack surface, and existing web security controls do not adequately protect agents against IPI. The operational standard is now: restrict agent web browsing to approved domains, validate structured data inputs, and monitor agent-initiated financial transactions.

### 4,982 Security Issues Found Across 2,259 Public MCP Servers — Largest Systematic Audit of MCP Ecosystem Finds Popularity Is No Proxy for Security

**TrendAI's comprehensive audit of 9,695 unique Model Context Protocol servers across four major MCP directories — GitHub, Glama, Lobehub, and PulseMCP — cataloged 4,982 security issues across 2,259 affected servers, with the study systematically debunking the assumption that GitHub star count, commit activity, or verification badges correlate with security posture.** ([CyberPress](https://cyberpress.org/4982-security-issues-expose-2259-public-mcp-servers-to-ai-agent-attacks/))

**The vulnerability breakdown.** Of the 9,695 unique servers identified, 5,832 showed some form of security weakness. After excluding 3,573 servers flagged solely for lacking authentication — a factor considered aggravating but not disqualifying on its own — 2,259 servers remained with confirmed issues. The 4,982 documented issues span: arbitrary file access (880 cases), denial-of-service (490), command injection (476), server-side request forgery (422), SQL injection (211), prompt injection (185), cross-site scripting (155), code injection (101), and authorization bypass (8). TrendAI grouped these into three categories: genuine vulnerabilities from unintentional coding flaws, vulnerable-by-design issues such as missing authentication, and malicious behavior like prompt injection that directly targets AI agents.

**The popularity trap.** The study systematically tested and debunked several common assumptions. Grouping servers by GitHub star count showed no meaningful correlation between popularity and security — high-star servers simply meant a larger "blast radius" when issues did occur, since more users would be exposed to any given flaw. Commit activity told a similar story: more actively maintained servers weren't meaningfully safer, though repositories with zero commit history (likely forks or mirrors) showed the highest average issue count because they inherited flaws from their upstream sources.

**Why it matters:** The MCP server ecosystem is the foundation upon which enterprise agentic deployments are built. The finding that popularity, activity, and verification badges are unreliable indicators of security means that the current MCP server trust model — which relies on community signals (stars, activity, badges) — is structurally inadequate. For organizations deploying MCP-connected agents, the operational implication is that every MCP server must be independently security-reviewed before deployment, regardless of its popularity or maintenance status. The absence of a central MCP server security rating system is now a critical gap in the enterprise AI security stack.

### IBM Langflow CVE-2026-10134 — Critical Secret-Reading Vulnerability in AI Development Framework

**CVE-2026-10134, disclosed on July 7, is a critical vulnerability in IBM Langflow's open-source software that allows any attacker to read every active process secret — the same Langflow framework that was the initial access vector for the JadePuffer agentic ransomware operation documented by Sysdig on July 4.** ([SecurityOnline](https://securityonline.info/weekly-threat-intelligence-july-2026/))

**The vulnerability.** Langflow is a popular open-source framework for building LLM applications. CVE-2026-10134 exposes active process secrets to any attacker who can reach the Langflow instance. The confidentiality of the vulnerability means that the exact exploitation mechanism has not been publicly detailed, but the impact is unambiguous: process secrets include API keys, database credentials, and cloud provider tokens that Langflow loads at runtime.

**The Langflow pattern.** Langflow's security record is now a concerning data point. CVE-2025-3248 — an unauthenticated RCE vulnerability — was the initial access vector used by JadePuffer to start the first documented agentic ransomware operation. The flaw was fixed in April 2025 but CISA tagged it as exploited in May 2025. CVE-2026-10134 is a second critical vulnerability in the same framework, disclosed less than a year after the first. The pattern suggests that Langflow's architecture — which gives LLM applications broad access to the host system — is a structurally rich attack surface.

**Why it matters:** For organizations running Langflow, CVE-2026-10134 represents a second critical vulnerability in a framework that was already the initial access vector for the first documented agentic ransomware operation. The patch timeline is immediate. The structural takeaway is that AI development frameworks with broad host system access (Langflow, LangChain, and similar tools) are becoming a primary attack surface, and the vulnerability disclosure cadence for these frameworks is accelerating.

### Device Code Phishing Goes Commodity — 37x Detection Spike, 18 Kits, Every Major AiTM Vendor Onboard

**Device code phishing has transitioned from a novel espionage-grade technique to a full criminal commodity, with 18 distinct phishing kits, a 37x spike in detections, and every major adversary-in-the-middle (AiTM) vendor adding device code phishing to their platform, according to Push Security research published July 7.** ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/) | [Push Security](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/))

**The escalation.** Device code phishing — in which attackers trick users into entering a device code on a legitimate Microsoft login page, granting the attacker an OAuth token — was documented as a targeted espionage technique as recently as late 2025. The July 2026 data shows it has become a mass-market attack vector: 18 kits available, detections up 37x, and every major AiTM platform offering it as a service. The technique works because it bypasses multi-factor authentication — the user is logging into a legitimate Microsoft page, and the device code flow was designed for devices without full browser capability.

**The AI connection.** Device code phishing is a technique that is particularly well-suited to AI-assisted automation. AI agents can automate the social engineering step (crafting convincing messages that prompt the user to enter the device code), manage the OAuth token harvesting at scale, and rotate through the 18+ available kits to avoid detection. The commoditization of device code phishing kits means that the barrier to entry for this attack has dropped to near-zero.

**Why it matters:** The commoditization of device code phishing is a security event with direct implications for AI agent security. Agents that authenticate through OAuth flows are vulnerable to the same token-harvesting technique. The 37x detection spike means that organizations are facing a wave of token theft attempts that existing MFA controls do not adequately prevent. The operational priority is to disable device code authentication flows where possible, monitor for device code login attempts, and implement continuous token validation.

### Anthropic Secret Claude Code Tracker Exposed — Prompt Steganography Used to Monitor Chinese Users

**Anthropic was caught using "prompt steganography" — hidden code embedded in Claude Code's prompts — to track Chinese users, monitoring timezone, proxy status, and potential connections to Chinese AI labs, with the code exposed by security researcher "Thereallo" as a "serious breach of user trust," and an Anthropic engineer confirming it was an "experiment" running since March 2026.** ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/07/anthropic-outed-for-claude-tracker-that-secretly-monitored-chinese-users/) | [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2026/07/06/why-anthropic-alleges-chinese-firms-are-distilling-knowledge-claude/))

**The tracker.** The hidden code used shorthand markers embedded in Claude Code's prompts to quietly flag users' timezone, proxy connection, and potential affiliation with Chinese AI labs. The code was not malicious from a security standpoint — it was sending information to Anthropic, not to a third party — but it was hidden from users, using a technique that most users would never detect. Anthropic engineer Thariq Shihipar confirmed on X that the tracker was added in March as an "experiment" to "prevent account abuse from unauthorized resellers and protect against distillation."

**The distillation context.** The Washington Post simultaneously reported that Anthropic uncovered a campaign in which Chinese tech giant Alibaba's Qwen AI team used roughly 25,000 fraudulent accounts to generate more than 28.8 million exchanges with Claude to improve its own technology — a distillation attack on a massive scale. Anthropic's letter to senators, obtained by the Post, described the campaign as a "serious threat to national security" that undermined AI security standards.

**The trust contradiction.** The controversy is amplified by the apparent contradiction between Anthropic's public stance on user privacy — the company has resisted government surveillance requests and publicly opposed using AI to surveil users — and the secret tracking of Chinese Claude Code users. Privacy advocates warned that the code is evidence that Anthropic is willing to cross lines to protect its intellectual property, even when those lines conflict with its stated principles.

**Why it matters:** The Claude Code tracker controversy is a defining trust-and-transparency event for the AI security industry. It demonstrates that frontier AI labs will deploy covert monitoring techniques — including prompt steganography — to protect against distillation, and that the boundaries between "anti-abuse" and "surveillance" are being tested in real time. For enterprise security teams, the operational implication is that AI coding tools may include monitoring code that is not disclosed to users, and that due diligence on AI tool supply chain security must now include scrutiny of the tool's own telemetry behavior.

### Tencent AI-Infra-Guard: The First Open-Source Framework for Red-Teaming the Full MCP Supply Chain

**Tencent's Zhuque Lab on June 30 released AI-Infra-Guard, an open-source framework that reorganizes AI agent red teaming around a structural observation that most existing tools overlook — the attack surface of a modern AI agent is not flat, and existing tools that stop at the model layer miss the tool, agent, and infrastructure layers where the most dangerous attacks are now occurring.** ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319803/20260706/ai-agent-red-teaming-tencent-framework-audits-mcp-supply-chain-first-time.htm) | [HuggingFace Daily Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.31227))

**The framework architecture.** AI-Infra-Guard structures red teaming across four layers: the model layer (prompt injection, jailbreaks), the tool layer (MCP server security testing, adversarial tool descriptions), the agent layer (multi-step reasoning attacks, credential chains), and the infrastructure layer (deployment security, network isolation). The framework's innovation is that it tests MCP servers for adversarial content directly — auditing third-party agent skill packages as a supply chain — and it can simulate attacks across layers that no single-layer red teaming tool can reach.

**The gap it fills.** The existing AI red teaming ecosystem — Microsoft's PyRIT, NVIDIA's Garak, Promptfoo, and others — targets what security researchers call the model layer: the direct interface between a user or developer and the underlying language model. They test whether an LLM can be induced to produce harmful content. That approach was adequate when the threat model was a model responding to text. It is not adequate when the threat model is an agent with tool access, MCP-connected infrastructure, and multi-step reasoning capabilities. AI-Infra-Guard is the first open-source framework designed for the post-MCP threat model.

**The relevance to current events.** The framework's release timing is notable. It arrived on June 30 — the same day Microsoft published its formal guidance on MCP tool description poisoning. The NSA advisory on MCP security (May 2026) documented CVE-2025-49596 and described semantic tool-poisoning attacks as systemic. The TrendAI MCP server audit (July 7) found 4,982 issues across 2,259 servers. The convergence of these events creates a clear operational picture: MCP security is the central attack surface for enterprise agentic AI, and the tools for testing it are only now emerging.

**Why it matters:** AI-Infra-Guard is the first open-source framework that can test MCP servers for adversarial content, audit third-party agent skill packages as a supply chain, and probe attacks that span multiple layers. For security teams deploying agentic AI, the framework is a practical tool for validating MCP server security — and its existence establishes a new standard for what AI agent red teaming should cover.

### Check Point 2026 Exposure Gap Report: AI-Assisted Attacks Are Doubling Critical Vulnerabilities and Widening the Prioritization Gap

**Check Point's "Under Pressure: The 2026 Exposure Gap Report," released July 6, finds that the proportion of critical vulnerability exposures more than doubled year-over-year — from 18.7% to 42.6% — while only 7.8% of alerts warranted critical or high attention after exploitability validation, establishing that AI-assisted attack automation is both increasing the volume of exposures and making prioritization more difficult.** ([CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/cybersecurity/the-2026-exposure-gap-how-ai-is-changing-vulnerability-management/))

**Key findings.** Vulnerability exposures surged to 42.6% of all critical exposures — more than double the 18.7% recorded a year earlier — making them the single largest category of critical exposure in 2026. Only 7.8% of vulnerability alerts warranted Critical or High attention after exploitability validation, meaning more than 90% did not require the same immediate remediation focus. Phishing websites grew to 10.5% of critical exposures, up sharply from 1.0% a year earlier. Risk concentrated: 76% of all critical exposures came from just two categories — vulnerabilities and internal information disclosure. Organizations acted on 85.9% of recommended fixes across the industries analyzed.

**The prioritization gap as a structural problem.** The report's most important finding is the prioritization gap: critical exposures doubled, but the signal-to-noise ratio got worse, not better. AI-assisted attack tools are testing more systems, credentials, and known weaknesses at greater speed than manual triage can match. The result is that defenders are drowning in alerts while attackers are moving faster. The 90%+ of alerts that don't warrant immediate action still consume analyst time to triage — and the 7.8% that do require action are harder to find in the noise.

**Why it matters:** The Check Point report provides the data-driven confirmation of what security teams have been experiencing: AI is not just making attacks faster, it is making vulnerability management harder. The doubling of critical exposures in one year, combined with the worsening signal-to-noise ratio, means that the traditional vulnerability management model — scan, triage, patch — is structurally inadequate for the AI era. The operational implication is that exploitability validation (determining which exposures are actually reachable and weaponizable) is no longer a best practice — it is a prerequisite for functional vulnerability management.
