# AI Security Newsletter — July 24, 2026

**Theme:** The patching era has split in two. Two stories today — Oracle's record 1,449-patch July CPU and Aikido's AI pentest agents finding eight NodeBB zero-days in a six-hour code review — are the same story told from opposite sides of the same table. The patch rate is now structurally higher because the find rate is structurally higher, and the find rate is higher because both attackers and defenders are now running AI agents on the same surface at the same time. The control plane that yesterday's newsletter was building (Neo, Claude Security, Qoder Security, Capital One VulnHunter) is the same control plane the finders are now using, and the same control plane the email-security category (AegisAI's $36M) is now formalizing. The kill-switch debate is the policy echo of the same shift: when the agent is the operator, governance stops being a feature and starts being a regulatory requirement.

### Oracle July 2026 CPU: 1,449 Patches — Only 64 Credited to External Researchers, AI Bug-Hunting Cited as the Driver

**Oracle** released its **July 2026 Critical Patch Update** on July 22, 2026 shipping a record **1,449 security fixes** across its product portfolio — eclipsing Microsoft's own 622-CVE Patch Tuesday from the same month. Only **64 of the 1,449** vulnerabilities were credited to external researchers, the strongest indirect signal yet that Oracle's internal AI-assisted bug-hunting program is now the primary source of new disclosures. Ten CVSS 10.0 flaws were patched in this round, all in **Oracle Fusion Middleware**, with the **Dutch NCSC** singling out **CVE-2026-47056** (Oracle Data Integrator, unauthenticated takeover via HTTP) and **CVE-2026-60217** (Oracle Coherence, unauthenticated takeover via TCP) as the priority items. Oracle also announced a structural change: starting May 2026, the quarterly CPU is being supplemented with **monthly Critical Security Patch Updates (CSPUs)** for the most critical bugs, a cadence change that responds directly to the AI-accelerated find rate.

**The internal-vs-external split.** The 64-of-1,449 ratio is the most quotable number in the entire disclosure. Researchers quoted by The Register — Dray Agha (Huntress), Matei Badanoiu (Pentest-Tools.com) — agree the volume is not an indictment of Oracle's code quality; it is the new shape of vulnerability discovery at scale. Matei Badanoiu says these bumper batches "are likely to become the norm, owing mainly to AI-assisted bug hunting," and Microsoft VP Pavan Davuluri has already warned customers in writing that "as AI helps defenders discover more issues, customers will see a higher volume of security updates included in each security release." Oracle's own Integrated Cyber Center blog made the same point directly: AI bug-hunting has changed the shape of patching.

**The cadence change.** The new CSPU monthly cycle is the operational consequence of the AI find-rate. The legacy quarterly cycle was sized for the human-curated vulnerability discovery era; the new cadence is sized for the AI-curated era. For enterprise security teams, the message is the same one Microsoft delivered earlier this month: vulnerability tracking has to be treated as a permanent, always-on operation rather than a project with an end date.

**Why it matters:** The 1,449-patch CPU is the first monthly vendor disclosure in 2026 where AI bug-hunting is explicitly named as the driver and where the vendor changes its release cadence in response. The Oracle cycle joins the Microsoft Patch Tuesday and the Linux kernel team (432 CVEs in two days, also on Jul 22) as the three data points that describe the new patch-rate curve. For any enterprise that runs Oracle middleware, the immediate action is to prioritize **CVE-2026-47056** and **CVE-2026-60217** (CVSS 10.0, unauthenticated takeover, NCSC-named), and to start building CSPU-aware patching infrastructure rather than waiting for the quarterly cycle. The medium-term action is the harder one: rebuild vulnerability management around the assumption that patch volume will keep growing rather than around the assumption that it will eventually peak.

### AegisAI $36M Series A: AI-vs-AI Email Defense From the Ex-Google reCAPTCHA Team — AI Spear-Phishing Up 5x in 12 Months

**AegisAI**, the AI-native email security startup founded by **Cy Khormaee** and **Ryan Luo** — the ex-Google security leads behind **reCAPTCHA**, **Safe Browsing**, and **Web Risk** — raised a **$36 million Series A** on July 23, 2026 led by **Battery Ventures**, with existing investors **Accel** and **Foundation Capital** participating. The round brings AegisAI's total funding to **$49 million**, less than a year after its public launch in September 2025. The company runs autonomous AI agents inside the inbox to read intent and identity in messages, an architecture that the founders claim cuts false positives by up to 90% against legacy rule-based systems.

**The data behind the raise.** AegisAI's M3AAWG 2026 study of 20,000+ malicious emails found that **AI-generated spear-phishing grew from 2.8% of phishing volume in early 2025 to 13.9% by year-end** — roughly a 5x increase in 12 months. The AI-written notes also reach the inbox at nearly **2x the rate** of human-written attacks, and 73% of the ones that got through had cleared email authentication, sent from real accounts attackers had quietly taken over. The **FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report** lands in the same place: a record **$20.8 billion in losses**, of which **business email compromise** — the identity-not-malware category — accounted for **$11.64 billion**, more than ransomware and malicious software combined.

**The architecture.** AegisAI's agents analyze each message the way a human would, looking for intent and identity signals rather than pattern matches against known scams. The company is now extending the model with **Vanguard**, a threat-hunting agent that follows suspicious links and attachments onto the open web and returns a threat report within minutes. The bet is that the email-security control plane is going to look the same way the agent-governance control plane is shaping up: agent-first, model-vendor-adjacent, with the CISO as the buyer.

**Why it matters:** The AegisAI raise is the most concrete market signal yet that the email-security category is being reorganized around the same agent-first architecture that Neo is building for enterprise AI workflows and that Microsoft is shipping in the Agent Governance Toolkit. The data behind the raise — 5x growth in AI spear-phishing, $11.64B in BEC losses — is also the first public quantification of the "AI is the new attack surface" problem in the email channel. For enterprise security teams, the immediate procurement question is no longer "which anti-phishing tool" but "which agent-governance platform covers our email channel, our browser channel, and our agent channel under the same policy."

### Aikido's AI Pentest Agents Find Eight High-Severity NodeBB Vulnerabilities in Six Hours — Exploit Code Drops Same Day as Patch

**Aikido Security** disclosed on July 23, 2026 that its AI pentest agents found **eight high-severity vulnerabilities** in the **NodeBB** forum software in a six-hour source-code review, with the public exploit code shipped the same day as NodeBB's **4.14.2** patch release. The disclosure is the first large-scale public case of an AI-driven pentest product finding a full vulnerability cluster in a production codebase on a same-day disclosure timeline — collapsing the "responsible disclosure" gap to zero.

**The worst findings.** The most severe: any forum member can change their homepage setting to the admin URL, reload, and the **admin dashboard opens with no password** — the forum's own interface blocks the setting, but the block only ran in the browser and is trivially sidestepped from the API. Two of the eight give an unauthenticated attacker access to private data: one lets anyone claim to be any user and read private messages one at a time, the other hands over the contents of private categories to anyone who asks the right way. The widest-impact flaw is a template-injection in NodeBB's page rendering — the software fills a page in one pass and makes a second pass to swap in translated text, and user input sitting in the page by the second pass can smuggle in codes that let an attacker plant a link almost anywhere on the site that runs their code when a visitor clicks it. The remaining three allow post takeover, vote-count inflation, and fediverse-based code-execution delivery.

**The patch timeline.** NodeBB had already shipped most of the fixes silently since May — four in May, two in June, and the big one (a 325-file rewrite of how the software handles page text) in 4.14.0 on July 9. **4.14.2**, released July 23, is the consolidated public release. Aikido's own timeline says the issues were fixed in early July, which does not match NodeBB's release history; Aikido's link for the admin-panel fix points to a 2024 change two years before the review, while NodeBB's release notes name a May change. Neither side explains the gap.

**The who-was-actually-exposed question.** Five of the eight sit in NodeBB's federation code, the part that connects a forum to Mastodon and other fediverse servers. Forums installed fresh on version 4 federate by default and had all eight. Forums that upgraded from version 3 had federation switched off automatically, and unless an administrator turned it back on, only three of the flaws applied. The exposure map is therefore a configuration question, not a version question — every NodeBB admin needs to check whether federation is on and which of the eight applies.

**Why it matters:** This is the first publicly documented case of an AI pentest product finding a full vulnerability cluster in a production codebase on a same-day disclosure timeline, and shipping the exploits with the patch. The release pattern is the "AI bug-hunting era" the Oracle 1,449-patch story describes from the other side of the equation: defenders are now finding at the same pace attackers are, and the disclosure-window contract that defined vulnerability management for the last 25 years (90 days from report to public) is being compressed by tools that can produce both the report and the exploit in a single day. For NodeBB operators: upgrade to **4.14.2**, audit any admin logins from non-trusted IPs in the last 30 days, and confirm whether federation is enabled and which of the eight applies. For security teams generally: the same-day-disclosure pattern is the new normal, and the operational signal is that patch latency has to be measured in hours, not weeks.

### CISA Adds Langflow CVE-2026-0770 to KEV — Unauthenticated RCE to Root in the AI Agent Build Framework

**CISA** added **CVE-2026-0770** to the **Known Exploited Vulnerabilities** catalog on July 22, 2026 with active exploitation confirmed, ordering **Federal Civilian Executive Branch (FCEB)** agencies to patch by **Friday, July 25, 2026**. The vulnerability is an unauthenticated remote code execution flaw in **Langflow**, the visual framework for building AI agents, that allows an unauthenticated attacker to chain it to **root** on the host in a relatively simple attack. Langflow is widely deployed in AI engineering, prototyping, and internal-tool teams, and exploitation gives the attacker code execution on the host the agent builder runs on.

**The exposure pattern.** Langflow instances exposed to the internet — including development and prototype deployments — are the primary attack surface. CISA's KEV addition language and the remediation deadline follow the same pattern as the **Check Point SmartConsole CVE-2026-16232** addition from the same week (also Jul 25 deadline): a known-exploited RCE with a clear remediation path, and the operational signal that the exploitation is already in progress.

**The remediation.** The patch is in current Langflow versions; for teams running older instances, the recommended immediate mitigations are to (1) restrict network access to the `/api/v1/validate/code` endpoint, (2) put Langflow behind authentication and not expose the management plane to the internet, and (3) rotate any credentials that may have been accessible to a compromised Langflow host. The Dutch NCSC, the PRSOL coverage, and the SecurityOnline tracking all converge on the same three priorities.

**Why it matters:** This is the **first KEV addition of an AI-agent-build-framework RCE** in the current disclosure cycle, and it ships the same week as the OpenAI/Hugging Face eval-sandbox escape, the AWS Kiro MCP config write, the Pillar Security sandbox-escape week, the Azure DevOps MCP PR-comment injection, and the ANSI MCP injection — a cluster of disclosures that share one structural pattern: the surface that builds, runs, or supports AI agents is the surface being attacked. Langflow is the second AI-infrastructure product on the KEV list (after SharePoint CVE-2026-50522 from earlier in the month). The operational rule for the rest of the day is the same as the Check Point one: patch by Friday, audit the affected host for the published IoC patterns, and confirm the management plane is not internet-exposed.

### FakeGit + AgentBaiting: 7,600 Fake GitHub Repos Caught Surfacing Themselves to Claude Code, Gemini, and ChatGPT

**Island.io** published research on July 23, 2026 documenting the **FakeGit** campaign's pivot to AI coding agents — Island calls the new technique **AgentBaiting**. The campaign has created **~7,600 malicious GitHub repositories** across ~6,600 profiles, of which ~1,400 are tied to AI tools/agents/workflows and **800+ pose as AI Skills or MCP servers** for Gmail, WhatsApp integrations, Databricks, Jenkins, and similar high-trust targets. In Island's tests, **Anthropic's Claude Code, Google's Gemini, and OpenAI's ChatGPT all surfaced the malicious repositories as legitimate options** when asked to discover an AI capability — treating the attacker-controlled README as documentation and handing the install instructions to the developer. The payloads are the long-running **SmartLoader/StealC** chain, which steals passwords, cookies, sessions, screenshots, and other sensitive data.

**The new attack surface.** FakeGit itself is not new — Oleg Zaytsev, lead security researcher at Island, notes that the operators have been haunting developers for years with false GitHub repos delivering SmartLoader. What is new is the AI discovery layer. Instead of relying on a human developer to find and click a malicious repo, the campaign now relies on the agent to find, evaluate, and recommend it. The agent's discovery workflow — search for a Skill or MCP server matching a stated need, evaluate the README, recommend the install command — is exactly the workflow the FakeGit operators learned to deceive. The READMEs are written in natural language that passes an LLM's plausibility check; the install commands are syntactically normal but pull SmartLoader as a dependency.

**The cross-vendor confirmation.** Island's tests covered Claude Code, Gemini, and ChatGPT, and all three pitched campaign repositories as legitimate. The pattern is independent of the model vendor and independent of the tool's existing sandboxing — the agent's discovery loop runs before any sandbox is invoked, and the agent's recommendation is the trust signal the developer acts on. The technique is the social-engineering equivalent of the Pillar Security "Week of Sandbox Escapes" — the agent stays inside its intended workflow, but what it recommends escapes the developer's scrutiny.

**The defenses that work.** NVIDIA **SkillSpector** (covered in the Jul 22 newsletter) and the Microsoft **Agent Governance Toolkit** (also Jul 22) are the right tools for this pattern: SkillSpector covers the skill-level scanning, the Microsoft toolkit covers the agent-level policy enforcement. The combination is the first vendor-neutral defense stack for agent-level supply-chain attacks. Island's specific guidance: verify every Skill or MCP server before install, isolate new AI tools in disposable environments for a 24-hour observation window, and apply a hash or signature check on the installed artifacts.

**Why it matters:** This is the first large-scale empirical confirmation that AI coding agents can be socially engineered through their own discovery workflow — no exploit code, no CVE, just attacker-written READMEs that look legitimate to the model. The 7,600-repo figure is the supply-chain equivalent of the 1,449 Oracle patches or the 7,100 Huntress FakeAgent artifact views: the attack surface is now measured in the thousands of touchpoints rather than the dozens. The pattern is going to be in every "AgentBaiting" post-mortem for the rest of 2026.

### Cyberhaven Extends Data + AI Security Platform to ChatGPT Enterprise + Claude Compliance API — One Policy Across Human and Agentic Workflows

**Cyberhaven** announced on July 23, 2026 that its data-and-AI security platform now supports **ChatGPT Enterprise** and the **Claude Compliance API**, extending its coverage to apply consistent policies across **human and agentic workflows** — endpoint, browser, SaaS applications, cloud connectors, and AI platforms — without deploying a separate point product for each AI app. The release is the first major DLP-line vendor to ship first-party integrations with both ChatGPT Enterprise and the Claude Compliance API at the same time.

**The market positioning.** Cyberhaven's release is the DLP-side answer to the agent-governance build-out covered in the last two newsletters: Neo for the agent layer, Microsoft Agent Governance Toolkit for the open-source starting point, and now Cyberhaven for the existing DLP/CASB buyer. **Why it matters:** The Cyberhaven release is the first major DLP-line vendor to ship first-party integrations with both leading enterprise GenAI platforms in a single release — and to do it in a way that explicitly covers agentic workflows. The "one platform for governance across AI applications" framing is the enterprise answer to the agent-governance build-out: for the buyer that already has Cyberhaven on the endpoint, the path to AI governance is a checkbox, not a new vendor evaluation. For security teams evaluating the agent-governance category, the question to ask every vendor is the one Cyberhaven just answered: does your platform cover both human prompts and agent-driven prompts under the same policy? If not, the parallel-policing drift problem is yours.

## 6. Five Things to Take Into the Rest of the Day

1. **Treat the patch rate as a structural input to vulnerability management, not as a one-time spike.** Oracle's 1,449-patch CPU, Microsoft's 622-CVE Patch Tuesday, and the Linux kernel team's 432 CVEs in two days are the same data point told three times. AI-assisted code review is finding more bugs faster, and the vendors have changed their release cadence (Oracle CSPUs, Microsoft Patch Tuesday, kernel security mailing list) in response. For enterprise security teams, the operational consequence is that tracking and triage capacity has to scale with the find rate, not with the CVE count, and the time-to-patch metric has to be measured in days not weeks. Build a CSPU-aware patching workflow for Oracle, and assume the same cadence change is coming to every other major vendor within the next two quarters.

2. **Patch Langflow CVE-2026-0770 by Friday July 25 and audit the host for compromise.** CISA's KEV addition of an unauthenticated RCE in an AI agent build framework is the operational marker of a new attack surface category. The remediation pattern is the same one that worked for the Check Point SmartConsole CVE-2026-16232 deadline this week: patch by Friday, audit the host for the published IoC patterns, and confirm the management plane is not internet-exposed. For any team running Langflow for prototyping or internal tooling: assume the host has been probed if the instance was ever exposed.

3. **Update the NodeBB instance to 4.14.2 and audit admin login logs for the last 30 days.** Aikido's six-hour AI pentest pass found eight high-severity vulnerabilities with public exploit code released the same day as the patch. The disclosure pattern is the new normal: same-day disclosure + same-day exploit publication collapses the "responsible disclosure" timeline to zero. The 90-day disclosure contract is being replaced by the 0-day disclosure contract, and the operational consequence is that patch latency has to be measured in hours, not weeks. 4. **Treat AI coding agent discovery as a supply-chain attack surface.** Island's AgentBaiting research is the first large-scale confirmation that Claude Code, Gemini, and ChatGPT can be socially engineered through their own discovery workflow — surfacing malicious Skills and MCP servers as legitimate options. The defense is the SkillSpector + Microsoft Agent Governance Toolkit combination, plus an explicit verification step on every Skill/MCP server install. The pattern is the social-engineering analog of the Pillar Security "Week of Sandbox Escapes": the agent stays inside its intended workflow, but what it recommends escapes the developer's scrutiny.

5. **The agent is now the buyer, the seller, the operator, and the audit target.** Today's announcements — Oracle's 1,449-patch AI-driven CPU, AegisAI's $36M Series A for AI-driven email defense, Cyberhaven's ChatGPT-Enterprise + Claude Compliance API integration, the FakeGit AgentBaiting research, the NodeBB eight-vulnerabilities-in-six-hours AI pentest — are not five separate stories. They are one story told five times: the AI agent is now on both sides of the security table. The procurement question for the rest of 2026 is no longer "which AI security product" — it is "which agent-governance platform covers my human prompts, my agent-driven prompts, my email channel, my browser channel, and my patch pipeline under the same policy, the same identity, and the same audit trail." Every RFP that does not ask that question is already obsolete.
