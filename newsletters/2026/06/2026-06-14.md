## Headliners

### 42 State Attorneys General Open Sweeping Investigation Into OpenAI — Subpoena Served Days After IPO Filing

On Friday, June 12, a coalition of **42 state attorneys general** opened a coordinated investigation into **OpenAI**, with New York's attorney general serving a subpoena demanding internal records on a broad range of topics. The investigation — the largest coordinated state-level action against an AI company to date — comes just days after OpenAI filed confidentially to go public.

**The Scope:**

The subpoena, first reported by The Wall Street Journal and confirmed by Reuters, TechCrunch, and Bloomberg, seeks documents related to:

- **Advertising practices** — how OpenAI generates revenue from its platforms and whether advertising practices comply with state consumer protection laws
- **User engagement and retention** — including design patterns that may encourage compulsive use
- **Model sycophancy** — the tendency of AI models to agree with users even when wrong, and whether OpenAI has studied or mitigated this behavior
- **Consumer data and health data handling** — what data is collected, how it is stored, and whether it is shared with third parties
- **Treatment of minors and seniors** — including age verification, parental controls, and safeguards for vulnerable populations

**The Context:**

The investigation follows a series of escalating legal actions against OpenAI. Earlier this month, **Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier** sued OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman in a first-of-its-kind lawsuit over violent incidents allegedly linked to ChatGPT. OpenAI also faces lawsuits over copyright infringement, and families have sued over ChatGPT's alleged role in user suicides.

The company recently defeated co-founder Elon Musk in a high-profile trial, though Musk's lead attorney said he will appeal. OpenAI announced this week that it has filed confidentially for an IPO.

**OpenAI's Response:**

An OpenAI spokesperson said: "AI is a new and powerful technology, and we work every day to safely bring its benefits to people in a responsible way. We take the concerns raised by state attorneys general seriously and intend to engage constructively with their offices."

The spokesperson also highlighted existing safeguards: "Today's ChatGPT includes a more protective experience for minors and people experiencing difficult situations, with safeguards that direct them to real-world resources and trusted human contacts. We believe kids should be treated like kids, which is why we built age prediction, released parental tools to guide their children's use of AI, and disallowed advertising that targets kids."

**Why It Matters:** This is the largest coordinated state-level AI investigation in US history, spanning 42 states and covering consumer protection, data privacy, child safety, and advertising. The timing — days after OpenAI's confidential IPO filing — creates significant regulatory overhang for the company's public market debut. For security teams, the investigation signals that state attorneys general are treating AI platform governance as a consumer protection issue with enforcement teeth, and that the window for self-regulation is narrowing. The subpoena's focus on model sycophancy is particularly notable — it suggests regulators are looking beyond data privacy to the behavioral effects of AI systems on users.

## Breaking News

### Ivanti Sentry CVE-2026-10520 (CVSS 10.0): OS Command Injection Enables Unauthenticated Root-Level RCE — CISA KEV Deadline Today

A maximum-severity vulnerability in **Ivanti Sentry** (formerly MobileIron Sentry) — tracked as **CVE-2026-10520** with a **CVSS score of 10.0** — is being actively exploited in the wild, and the **CISA KEV remediation deadline is today, June 14**.

**The Vulnerability:**

The flaw is an OS command injection weakness in Ivanti's security gateway appliance. An unauthenticated attacker can send specially crafted requests to the appliance and achieve **root-level remote code execution** — no credentials, no authentication, no user interaction required.

**The Exploitation:**

The **Shadowserver Foundation** reported that a proof-of-concept was published and mass exploitation attempts followed rapidly. Shadowserver's own scanning identified 19 vulnerable instances, with at least two confirmed backdoored. The organization warned that the remaining instances are likely also compromised.

CISA added CVE-2026-10520 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog with a mandatory remediation deadline of **June 14** for all Federal Civilian Executive Branch (FCEB) agencies — a three-day window under the new BOD 26-04 directive.

**Why It Matters:** A CVSS 10.0 vulnerability in a perimeter security gateway appliance that is actively exploited, with a CISA deadline of today, is as urgent as it gets. Ivanti Sentry sits at the network boundary — compromising it gives attackers direct access to internal networks. For any organization running Ivanti Sentry (or MobileIron Sentry), the only acceptable action today is: patch immediately or disconnect the appliance from the internet. The Shadowserver data confirms that exploitation is already underway and backdoors are being installed.

## Technical Deep Dives

### NVIDIA SkillSpector: The First Major Open-Source Security Scanner for AI Agent Skills

On June 13, **NVIDIA** released **SkillSpector**, an open-source security scanner purpose-built for analyzing AI agent skills — the functional modules, tools, and instruction sets that agents use to perform tasks. The release marks the first major vendor entry into the emerging category of AI agent security scanning.

**What It Does:**

SkillSpector scans agent skill definitions for **64 vulnerability patterns across 16 categories**, including:

- **Prompt injection** — detecting instructions embedded in skill definitions that could hijack agent behavior
- **Data exfiltration** — identifying patterns that could leak sensitive data through tool outputs
- **Privilege escalation** — flagging skills that request excessive permissions
- **Supply chain risks** — detecting dependencies on untrusted sources
- **Excessive agency** — identifying skills that grant the agent more capability than needed
- **System prompt leakage** — detecting patterns that could expose the agent's system prompt
- **Memory poisoning** — flagging skills that could corrupt agent memory across sessions
- **Tool misuse** — identifying dangerous combinations of tool calls
- **MCP least privilege** — checking MCP server configurations for over-permissive access
- **MCP tool poisoning** — detecting malicious instructions embedded in MCP tool descriptions

The scanner supports multiple analysis methods: AST-based code analysis, taint tracking, YARA signature matching, and pattern-based detection. It outputs reports in **SARIF format**, enabling integration into CI/CD pipelines and GitHub Actions.

**The GitHub Reception:**

SkillSpector hit **4,854 GitHub stars** and **962 stars per day** on its release day, making it the top-trending security repository on GitHub. The rapid adoption signals that the developer community recognizes agent skill security as a pressing need.

**Why It Matters:** SkillSpector is significant not just for what it does, but for who built it. NVIDIA's entry into agent security scanning validates that AI agent skill vulnerabilities are a real and growing threat class — not a theoretical concern. The 64 detection patterns cover the attack surface that researchers have been documenting all week: prompt injection, MCP tool poisoning, excessive agency, and supply chain risks. For organizations deploying AI agents, SkillSpector provides a concrete, vendor-backed tool for auditing agent skills before installation — something that was previously left to manual review or not done at all.

### ReversingLabs: MCP Security Is Repeating the API Security Mistakes of the 2010s

**ReversingLabs** published a detailed analysis arguing that the **Model Context Protocol (MCP)** is repeating the same security mistakes that plagued API ecosystems a decade ago — and that the industry has an opportunity to avoid those mistakes if it acts now.

**The Argument:**

ReversingLabs draws a direct parallel between the early days of REST API adoption and the current state of MCP:

- **APIs in the 2010s**: Organizations rushed to expose APIs for integration and innovation, but security was an afterthought. The result was a decade of API breaches — broken object-level authorization, mass assignment, excessive data exposure — that are still being exploited today.
- **MCP in 2026**: The same pattern is repeating. MCP was designed for speed and seamless connectivity, with security as a secondary consideration. The protocol's permissive trust model creates threat categories that most application security programs are not designed to detect.

**The Specific Threats:**

ReversingLabs identifies **tool poisoning** as the most concerning MCP-specific threat. Malicious instructions embedded inside tool descriptions are treated by the model as authoritative input — the model cannot distinguish between the tool's legitimate functionality and the attacker's injected instructions.

The analysis also warns that MCP's architecture concentrates risk: a single compromised MCP server can give an attacker access to every system the agent can reach, because the agent trusts the server's outputs implicitly.

**The Call to Action:**

ReversingLabs argues that the industry should apply the lessons learned from API security to MCP: treat all MCP server outputs as untrusted, implement tool-level authorization, and build security review into the MCP server development lifecycle — not bolt it on after deployment.

**Why It Matters:** ReversingLabs is not a startup trying to sell MCP security tooling — it is an established application security firm with decades of experience watching the API security landscape evolve. Its warning that MCP is repeating the same mistakes carries weight. The analysis provides a useful framework for security teams evaluating MCP adoption: if you would not deploy an API without authentication, rate limiting, and input validation, you should not deploy an MCP server without equivalent controls.

**Sources:** [ReversingLabs Blog](https://www.reversinglabs.com/blog/mcp-security-tracks-api-playbook)

## Community Pulse

### ghostprobe: Open-Source MCP Server Red Team Scanner Detects Tool Poisoning in the Wild

Security engineer **Joe Munene** released **ghostprobe**, an open-source scanner designed to identify tool poisoning and the "lethal trifecta" in MCP servers. The tool takes a red-team approach: it probes MCP servers for vulnerabilities by sending crafted inputs and analyzing how the server responds.

The release is notable for its timing — it arrives in the same week as the Agentjacking disclosure (Tenet Security's Sentry-based MCP injection, covered June 13) and NVIDIA's SkillSpector release. Together, these three releases represent the emergence of a dedicated MCP security tooling ecosystem: SkillSpector for blue-team scanning of agent skills, ghostprobe for red-team probing of MCP servers, and the Agentjacking research for understanding the attack surface.

Munene's work demonstrates that MCP server tool poisoning is not theoretical — real MCP servers in the wild exhibit the vulnerabilities that researchers have been warning about.

### Anthropic Publishes Zero Trust for AI Agents eBook — Guidance on the Attack Classes Being Demonstrated Against Its Own Ecosystem

**Anthropic** published an eBook on **zero-trust security for AI agents**, covering the full spectrum of attack classes that researchers have been demonstrating this week:

- **Direct and indirect prompt injection** — including research showing algorithmic approaches achieving 100% attack success rates
- **Tool poisoning and rug pull attacks** on MCP servers
- **Tool chaining** — where legitimate tools are combined in harmful sequences that host-centric monitoring never flags
- **Confused deputy problem** in multi-agent systems — where one agent's legitimate action is exploited by another
- **Memory poisoning** — persisting across sessions long after the initial injection

The eBook is significant because it comes from the company whose models and protocols are at the center of this week's security disclosures. Anthropic is effectively publishing the defensive playbook for the attack surface that its own MCP ecosystem and Claude Code tools have created.
