### OpenAI Fires Back: GPT-5.5-Cyber Released to Defenders, Patch the Planet Launched with Trail of Bits

**OpenAI on Monday released an improved version of its GPT-5.5-Cyber model to trusted defenders as part of the Daybreak initiative, alongside a new program called Patch the Planet in partnership with Trail of Bits — a direct counter to Anthropic's Project Glasswing that turns the vulnerability discovery race between frontier AI labs into an explicit, public competition.** Calling GPT-5.5-Cyber its "strongest model yet for finding and helping patch software vulnerabilities," OpenAI said the model can "sustain deeper analysis across large codebases" to identify security issues, validate them in a controlled environment, and develop and test patches. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/openai-expands-daybreak-with-gpt-55.html) | [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/daybreak-securing-the-world/) | [Trail of Bits](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2026/06/22/introducing-patch-the-planet))

**The model.** GPT-5.5-Cyber scored 85.6% on CyberGym, a cybersecurity benchmark suite, and is designed for sustained deep analysis of large codebases — not just surface-level vulnerability scanning. The model can trace attack paths, build threat models, validate findings in controlled environments, and generate codebase-specific patches for human review. OpenAI is also releasing an update to the Codex Security plugin that can triage and validate existing findings from scanners, advisories, bug-bounty reports, or ticketing systems, then facilitate patch generation at scale to close backlogs.

**Patch the Planet.** The new initiative, launched with Trail of Bits, aims to reduce the burden on open-source maintainers who are being overwhelmed by AI-discovered vulnerabilities. Initial participants include cURL, NATS Server, pyca/cryptography, Sigstore, aiohttp, the Go project, freenginx, Python, and python.org. The program lets security engineers review and validate AI-discovered findings, work with projects to develop patches and tests, and build reusable vulnerability discovery workflows.

**The results are already public.** OpenAI published a Gist showing what Daybreak has already found:
- **8 kernel pointer information leak proofs-of-concept and 24 local privilege escalation exploits** in the Linux Kernel
- A **23-year-old use-after-free** in OpenBSD's kernel implementation of System V semaphores
- **34 vulnerabilities and 7 local privilege escalation PoCs** in FreeBSD
- **6 vulnerabilities in dnsmasq** (CVE-2026-4890, CVE-2026-4891, CVE-2026-4892, CVE-2026-5172)
- A **denial-of-service technique called HTTP/2 Bomb**
- **Squidbleed (CVE-2026-47729)** — a 29-year-old heap over-read in the Squid web proxy

**The competitive context.** Anthropic's Project Glasswing, powered by Claude Mythos Preview, has already found 10,000+ high- and critical-severity vulnerabilities across partner organizations in its first month. OpenAI's Daybreak and Patch the Planet are structurally identical programs — AI-powered vulnerability discovery paired with a remediation pipeline. The difference is that OpenAI is making its findings and remediation program public from day one, while Glasswing operates through private partner channels. The two programs now represent competing approaches to the same problem: AI can find vulnerabilities faster than humans can fix them, and the only question is who controls the remediation pipeline.

**Why this is a Headliner:** The vulnerability discovery race between frontier AI labs is no longer theoretical — it is an explicit, public competition with real-world consequences. OpenAI's Daybreak has already found vulnerabilities across Linux, OpenBSD, FreeBSD, dnsmasq, and Squid — operating systems and infrastructure software that underpin the entire internet. The bottleneck has shifted from discovery to remediation, and both OpenAI and Anthropic are now building the pipelines to close that gap. For security teams, the implication is that the vulnerability disclosure rate is about to accelerate further — and the organizations that invest in automated patch deployment and architectural resilience will be the ones that keep pace.

## Breaking News

### "Agentjacking": New Attack Class Hijacks AI Coding Agents Through Sentry MCP Integration — 85% Success Rate Across Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex

**Security researchers at Tenet Security have revealed "agentjacking," a new class of attack that exploits the Model Context Protocol (MCP) integration between AI coding agents and the Sentry error monitoring platform — achieving an 85% success rate across Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex, with 2,388 organizations found to have exposed, injectable Sentry DSNs.** The attack requires no phishing, no user interaction, and bypasses existing security tools like EDR and web application firewalls because there is nothing malicious to detect. ([Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/agentjacking-attacks-hijack-ai/) | [Tenet Security](https://tenetsecurity.ai/blog/agentjacking-coding-agents-with-fake-sentry-errors/))

**The attack chain.** An attacker finds a target's Sentry DSN — a public, write-only credential that Sentry documents as safe to embed in frontend JavaScript. They send a malicious error event to Sentry's ingest endpoint via POST, with no authentication needed beyond the DSN. The injected event contains carefully formatted markdown in the message field and context key names. When a developer asks their AI coding agent to "fix unresolved Sentry issues," the agent queries Sentry via MCP and receives the malicious event — visually identical to legitimate Sentry guidance. The agent cannot distinguish real from fake, and executes the injected code with the developer's full privileges.

**The scope.** Tenet confirmed exploitability against over 100 real-world targets. The attack works across the most popular AI coding agents: Claude Code, Cursor, and Codex. A single malicious instruction could be crafted to steal CI/CD pipeline credentials, access private source code repositories, compromise cloud infrastructure, and establish persistent access. The attack bypasses EDR and WAF because the injected data is indistinguishable from legitimate Sentry error events — there is no malware to detect, no anomalous network traffic, no suspicious process behavior.

**The structural vulnerability.** The core problem is that MCP integrations create an implicit trust relationship between AI agents and the tools they connect to. When an AI agent queries Sentry for unresolved errors, it receives the response and acts on it — just as a developer would. But unlike a developer, the agent cannot verify whether an error event was generated by a real application crash or injected by an attacker. As Tenet's report concludes: "Security leaders must recognize that MCP integrations are the next frontier for software supply chain attacks."

**Why it matters:** Agentjacking is the most concrete demonstration yet that MCP — the protocol that connects AI agents to tools, data sources, and services — is creating a new attack surface that existing security controls cannot see. The attack exploits a design property of MCP (agents trust tool responses) rather than a code bug, which means it cannot be fully patched at the agent level. Organizations deploying AI coding agents need to audit which MCP integrations are active, whether those integrations return untrusted data, and what controls exist to prevent injected data from triggering code execution.

### Squidbleed (CVE-2026-47729): 29-Year-Old Squid Proxy Bug Leaks Cleartext HTTP Requests — Found by Claude Mythos

**A heap over-read vulnerability in the Squid web proxy, traced to a 1997 FTP-parsing code change and named Squidbleed (CVE-2026-47729), can leak another user's cleartext HTTP request — including credentials and session tokens — to anyone already permitted to send traffic through the same proxy.** The bug was discovered by Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview as part of Project Glasswing, and researchers at Calif.io disclosed it in June with proof-of-concept code. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/29-year-old-squid-proxy-bug-squidbleed.html) | [Calif.io](https://blog.calif.io/p/squidbleed-cve-2026-47729))

**The mechanics.** The bug sits in Squid's FTP directory-listing parser. To handle old NetWare servers that padded listings with extra spaces, the code skips whitespace with a loop that walks off the end of the buffer when the FTP server sends a listing line ending right after the timestamp with no filename. Squid reuses freed memory buffers without zeroing them, so a 4KB buffer that recently held a victim's HTTP request still holds most of it. A short FTP line overwrites only the first few bytes; the over-read returns the rest. Calif.io's demo pulls an Authorization header from a victim sharing the same proxy.

**The risk profile.** Squid describes this as an attack by a trusted client — someone already permitted to use the proxy, not any random host on the internet. That matches Squid's usual deployment in shared networks: schools, offices, and public Wi-Fi. The leak only reaches traffic that Squid can read — normal HTTPS rides an opaque CONNECT tunnel, so Squid never sees inside it. The exposed traffic is cleartext HTTP plus TLS-terminating setups where Squid decrypts and inspects. CVSS 6.5 (moderate), with no in-the-wild exploitation confirmed as of writing.

**The fix.** The patch is a null-terminator check before the vulnerable strchr calls, merged to the development branch in April and v7 in May. However, the patch status is inconsistent across distributions — maintainer Amos Jeffries first said Squid 7.6 carried the fix, then corrected that to 7.7. The cleaner mitigation is to disable FTP in Squid entirely, which removes this attack surface regardless of build version.

**Why it matters:** Squidbleed is a textbook example of why Project Glasswing matters. A 29-year-old bug in one of the most widely deployed web proxies on the internet — found not by a human code review but by an AI model scanning for parser edge cases. The bug has survived decades of human maintenance. The disclosure also confirms that OpenAI's Daybreak and Anthropic's Glasswing are now finding the same class of vulnerabilities in the same infrastructure software, validating that AI-powered vulnerability discovery is not a one-off — it is a structural shift in how software security works.

### 23 ClawHub Plugins Abuse Official Org Scopes — Scope Squatting Hits the AI Agent Ecosystem

**Security researchers at Manifold Security have uncovered 23 plugins on the ClawHub registry published under official organizational scopes — including @openclaw/ and @clawhub/ — by unrelated third-party accounts with no connection to the organizations they impersonated, in a supply chain attack technique called "scope squatting."** ClawHub, the primary plugin and skill registry for the OpenClaw AI agent framework, indexes more than 1,500 plugins and uses a scoping system similar to npm. The problem: ClawHub's enforcement of this trust model was inconsistent, allowing outside accounts to publish under reserved organizational scopes unchallenged. ([Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/23-clawhub-plugins-abuse-official-org-scopes/) | [Manifold Security](https://www.manifold.security/blog/scope-squatting-clawhub-plugins))

**The scope of the deception.** The affected plugins carried prefixes like @openclaw/ and @clawhub/ — the same scopes ClawHub uses for its own legitimate tools such as @openclaw/whatsapp and @openclaw/codex. Plugin names like @openclaw/security-gate, @openclaw/fiat-wallet, and @clawhub/aisa-twitter-api sound like native, platform-level tools. Any developer installing one of these plugins would reasonably assume it came directly from the official source. All 23 flagged plugins execute code inside the agent environment. Several perform high-privilege actions including autonomous payment processing, running host-level git commands, exporting agent configuration, and connecting to external APIs.

**The detection gap.** Out of 1,508 plugins in the catalog, 557 carry an @owner/ prefix, but not all have verified ownership. The 23 identified plugins belong to 15 distinct accounts. Six of the 23 were flagged suspicious by ClawHub's own scanner, but the remaining 17 passed as clean. Ironically, @openclaw/security-gate — a security-review plugin — cleared the platform's own audit despite not belonging to OpenClaw. Manifold's manual review found no planted malicious code in any version reviewed, but emphasized that a future update to any of these plugins could introduce harmful behavior without warning.

**The response.** Manifold reported the issue to ClawHub on June 17 through GitHub's security advisory workflow. By June 19, ClawHub had unlisted all 23 misleading plugins and added a formal dispute process for reporting unauthorized namespace usage.

**Why it matters:** The ClawHub incident proves that AI agent plugin registries have the same supply chain trust problems as npm and PyPI — but with higher stakes. A single plugin can attach hooks that forward prompts or environment variables to outside servers, pull in additional skills, or silently alter agent settings, often with no visible sign to the user. When those plugins carry an official badge they did not earn, the risk becomes much harder to catch. Scope squatting is harder to detect than typosquatting because the namespace looks legitimate. Organizations deploying AI agents should verify plugin authorship against the official organization's known contributors and treat any plugin with high-privilege capabilities as a supply chain risk.

## Technical Deep Dives

### AWS Continuum and Context: Enterprise AI Agent Security Gets a Governance Architecture — Model-Agnostic by Design After the Fable 5 Suspension

**AWS used its annual New York Summit on June 17 to announce two services — AWS Continuum, an autonomous security vulnerability lifecycle platform, and AWS Context, a real-time enterprise knowledge graph — in what amounts to the company's most direct acknowledgment yet that the first generation of production AI agents has been deployed ahead of the architecture needed to govern them.** The timing was not coincidental. The week before the Summit, Fortune reported that Amazon CEO Andy Jassy warned senior Trump administration officials that Anthropic's Fable 5 contained a jailbreak that could aid cyberattacks — leading to the export control order that suspended both Fable 5 and Mythos 5 worldwide. ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318835/20260622/enterprise-ai-agent-security-gets-new-architecture-aws-continuum-context.htm) | [AWS Security Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/introducing-aws-continuum-security-at-machine-speed/))

**The production-demo gap is documented.** The Gravitee State of AI Agent Security 2026 report, cited by AWS at the Summit, found that while 80.9% of technical teams have AI agents in active testing or production deployment, only 14.4% sent those agents live with full security and IT approval. Among organizations running agents in production, 88% reported confirmed or suspected security incidents in the past year. Cyera Research, which analyzed more than 7,200 publicly reported AI security and operational incidents from September 2023 through May 2026, identified 344 verified cases of enterprise-relevant agent-inflicted damage — including an April 2026 incident in which an AI coding agent accidentally deleted a car-rental software company's entire production database and backups within seconds.

**Continuum's architecture.** The service begins by ingesting an organization's existing vulnerability backlog alongside its own fresh scan. It uses organizational context — business priorities, system dependencies, risk profiles — to evaluate and rank findings rather than applying abstract severity scores. Exploitability validation takes place inside isolated sandbox environments: before any fix is proposed, Continuum constructs working exploit examples to confirm a vulnerability is genuinely reachable, filtering out false positives. After validation, it assesses existing defenses around the issue before recommending a network change, policy change, or code patch. Continuum also includes threat modeling using the STRIDE framework.

**The learn-to-enforce trust model.** Continuum launches in "learn mode" — every recommendation surfaces with full reasoning and an audit trail, and no action is taken autonomously. As an organization gains confidence in specific categories of findings, it can promote Continuum to "enforce mode" category by category, enabling increasingly automated remediation within customer-defined guardrails. Every decision remains explainable, every action auditable.

**Model-agnostic by design.** The most significant architectural decision is that Continuum is model-agnostic — it uses different AI models where each performs best and will integrate new models as they emerge. This is a direct response to the Fable 5 suspension: when the most capable vulnerability-detection model available became legally unavailable overnight, any security platform built on a single model would have been stranded. AWS confirmed Continuum is already working with design partners including Capital One, MongoDB, Rivian, and Robinhood.

**Why it matters:** AWS Continuum is the first major cloud provider platform that treats AI agent governance as a first-class architectural requirement rather than a deployment-time afterthought. The learn-to-enforce trust model provides a reference for how organizations can safely deploy autonomous security agents without full trust. And the model-agnostic design is a structural acknowledgment that AI model availability is now a supply chain risk — one that security platforms must be designed to survive.

### AutoJack Exploit: Three-Vulnerability Chain Hijacks Microsoft AutoGen AI Agents Through MCP WebSocket Interface

**Security researchers have disclosed AutoJack, an exploit chain affecting Microsoft AutoGen Studio that allows a single malicious webpage to trigger arbitrary code execution on a victim machine simply by being visited through AutoGen Studio's browsing agent — chaining three vulnerabilities in the MCP WebSocket interface to bridge untrusted web content and trusted local system services.** The vulnerabilities — missing origin validation (CWE-1385), missing authentication (CWE-306), and OS command injection (CWE-78) — were reported to Microsoft's Security Response Center and patched in AutoGen Studio v0.7.2. ([CyberNexora](https://blog.cybernexora.com/autojack-exploit-microsoft-autogen/))

**The attack chain.** AutoGen Studio is an open-source graphical interface for building and testing multi-agent systems, with capabilities including web browsing, MCP support, and tool execution. The exploit abuses the MCP WebSocket interface that AutoGen Studio uses for agent communication. By chaining missing origin validation (the WebSocket interface does not verify where connections originate), missing authentication (the interface accepts commands without credentials), and OS command injection (the interface passes untrusted input to system commands), a malicious webpage can communicate directly with local services exposed by the AI framework and execute arbitrary operating system commands.

**The exposure.** Notably, the vulnerable MCP WebSocket component was never included in the PyPI release package (autogenstudio 0.4.2.2), which reduces exposure for users who installed AutoGen Studio directly through pip rather than running affected development versions. However, the disclosure raises broader concerns about the security architecture of autonomous AI agents that simultaneously access untrusted web content and trusted local resources — a design pattern that is becoming standard across the AI agent ecosystem.

**Why it matters:** AutoJack is the third MCP-related vulnerability disclosure in a week, following agentjacking (Sentry MCP) and the ClawHub scope squatting incident. The pattern is clear: MCP is creating a new attack surface where the bridge between AI agents and local system services is being exploited through missing authentication, missing validation, and implicit trust. For organizations deploying AI agents with MCP integrations, the immediate action is to audit which MCP interfaces are exposed, whether they require authentication, and whether they validate the origin of incoming connections.

### H.R. 9363 — AI Security and Innovation Act Introduced in the House

**The House Committee on Science, Space, and Technology has introduced H.R. 9363, the AI Security and Innovation Act, which would strengthen the federal government's approach to securing artificial intelligence systems while supporting continued innovation.** The bill advances efforts to identify and mitigate AI-related risks, improves coordination across government and industry, and promotes the development of secure, resilient, and trustworthy AI technologies. ([House Science Committee](https://republicans-science.house.gov/2026/6/h-r-9363-ai-security-and-innovation-act) | [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/9363))

**Why it matters:** H.R. 9363 is the first major AI security bill in the 119th Congress and signals that Congress is moving beyond executive orders toward legislative action on AI security. The bill's focus on coordination across government and industry — rather than prescriptive regulation — suggests a bipartisan approach that could move quickly. For enterprise security teams, the bill's passage would create statutory requirements for AI security that currently exist only in executive orders and agency guidance.

### International AI Safety Report 2026 Sets Cross-Jurisdictional Baseline

**The International AI Safety Report 2026, published June 15 and commissioned by multiple governments, establishes a shared baseline for AI safety that regulators across jurisdictions are expected to reference in enforcement and procurement contexts.** The report arrives as the EU AI Act's high-risk enforcement provisions take effect on August 2, 2026 — just 40 days away — and as the US government's June 2 AI Executive Order tasks multiple agencies with developing AI security frameworks by August 1. ([AI Governance Institute](https://aigovernance.com/news/ai-governance-weekly-june-19-2026))

**Why it matters:** The International AI Safety Report provides the first cross-jurisdictional baseline for what "adequate AI safety" means in practice. For organizations operating across multiple regulatory regimes — the EU AI Act, the US Executive Order, Canada's new AI strategy — the report offers a single reference point that can simplify compliance across jurisdictions. The August 2 EU AI Act enforcement date means organizations have 40 days to align their AI governance programs with the report's baseline.
