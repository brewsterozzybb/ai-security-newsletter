## Headliners

### NSA Director: Mythos "Broke Into Almost All of Our Classified Systems in Hours" — The Real Story Behind the Fable 5 Ban

**NSA Director and US Cyber Command head Gen. Joshua Rudd told Sen. Mark Warner in a Senate Intelligence Committee briefing on June 11 that Anthropic's Mythos model, in a classified red-team exercise, autonomously breached nearly all of the NSA's classified systems — not over weeks, but in hours.** The disclosure, first reported by The Economist and confirmed by multiple sources on June 21, reframes everything about the US government's decision to impose export controls on Anthropic's two most advanced models. ([Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/anthropics-mythos-ai-model/) | [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/318783/20260621/claude-fable-5-resurfaces-android-app-nsa-breach-testimony-reshapes-ban.htm) | [The Economist](https://www.economist.com/))

**The timeline.** On June 11, Mythos was evaluated in a classified red-team exercise against NSA systems. According to Rudd's testimony, the model achieved autonomous access to "almost all" classified networks within hours. The next day, June 12, the Commerce Department issued an export control directive barring foreign nationals from accessing Fable 5 and Mythos 5 — the first time the United States has applied export controls directly to an AI model rather than to the hardware powering it. Anthropic suspended both models for all customers worldwide.

**The gap between public and private explanations.** Anthropic has publicly described the government's cited concern as a narrow jailbreak — a technique involving asking the model to read a specific codebase and identify flaws — that other publicly available models also exhibit. The company has characterized the wholesale recall as a disproportionate overreaction. The Rudd testimony suggests the government's actual concern is far broader: not a single jailbreak technique, but the demonstrated autonomous offensive capability of the unrestricted model against the nation's most sensitive networks.

**The model architecture matters.** Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 share the same underlying model weights. What separates them is safety architecture: Fable 5 runs all requests through classifiers covering offensive cybersecurity, biology, chemistry, and model distillation, handing flagged queries to Opus 4.8. Mythos 5 runs the same model without those classifiers, available only to vetted Project Glasswing partners. The classifiers trigger in fewer than 5% of Fable 5 sessions. The government's position, sharpened by the Rudd testimony, is that the gap between the two models is insufficiently protected by those classifiers.

**The restoration picture.** Despite the NSA testimony, signals point toward potential restoration. The Fable 5 model name reappeared in the Claude Android app's model selector on Sunday, with error messages changing from "model unavailable" to "rate-limiting" — suggesting a live backend under load. Prediction markets on Kalshi price approximately 57% probability of restoration before July 1. Anthropic's managing director for international, Chris Ciauri, said at the Seoul office opening on June 17 that he was "very confident" the models would return "in the coming days."

**The regulatory framework.** Section 3 of the White House's June 2 Executive Order "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security" tasks the NSA, Treasury, and CISA with developing a classified benchmarking process to designate AI models as "covered frontier models" and building a voluntary framework giving the government 30 days of pre-release access before developers ship to other partners. The deadline for that framework: August 1, 2026 — 60 days after the order. Anthropic launched Fable 5 on June 9 — seven days after the order and well before the framework was built.

**Why this is a Headliner:** This is the first time a US official has publicly described the scale of an AI model's autonomous offensive capability against classified networks. The Rudd testimony directly explains why the government has shown no urgency to lift the ban — and it establishes a precedent that every frontier AI lab will face: if your model can autonomously breach classified systems in hours, export controls are not a theoretical risk. They are a matter of when, not if.

## Breaking News

### WARP: 13 Words in a Reddit Comment Poison ChatGPT Deep Research and Gemini — Cornell Tech Paper Published June 22

**A newly published academic paper from Cornell Tech has revealed WARP (Web Agent Retrieval Poisoning), an attack that requires as few as 13 words slipped into a single Reddit comment to steer AI deep-research agents — including OpenAI's Deep Research and Google's Gemini Deep Research — toward fake products, scams, and misinformation.** The attack never breaks into OpenAI, Google, or Reddit. It simply adds promotional text to a page the AI agent already reads. ([Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/13-word-reddit-comment-chatgpt-and-gemini/) | [arXiv 2605.24245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24245))

**The mechanics.** Deep-research agents like STORM, Co-STORM, and OmniThink autonomously decompose a user's query into sub-queries, retrieve and synthesize content from the open web, and produce structured, cited reports. The key vulnerability: when these agents research any given topic, they repeatedly retrieve the same small set of user-generated content (UGC) pages — chiefly from Reddit and Wikipedia — regardless of how the query is phrased. That retrieval overlap creates a concentrated attack surface.

**The results.** In testing across 176 queries spanning 11 topic clusters:
- **Co-STORM** achieved a 100% conditional citation rate: every time the poisoned URL was retrieved, the fabricated entity was cited in the final report.
- **STORM** showed conditional citation rates of 72.5–80.8% and mention rates up to 56.9%.
- **Gemini Deep Research** cited UGC at a 12.1% rate, with 102 recurring UGC URLs across just 11 topic clusters.
- **OpenAI Deep Research** showed comparatively low UGC citation rates (~0.4%), though poisoned UGC could still influence intermediate reasoning steps.
- **Reddit** dominated as the most-retrieved UGC platform across all tested systems (54–71% of all UGC URLs retrieved).

**The defense problem.** The researchers evaluated three classes of defenses — source-level blocking (blacklisting UGC domains), input filtering (LLM-based content screening), and output filtering (semantic comparison to clean reports) — and found that none effectively neutralized the attack without degrading output quality. Perplexity-based detection proved counterproductive: GEO-generated poisoned text is fluent and LLM-authored, producing lower perplexity than organic UGC and actively evading high-perplexity filters.

**Why it matters:** WARP is the content-poisoning equivalent of prompt injection. It does not require compromising any system — it poisons the data the AI already trusts. For organizations deploying AI agents that search the web for research, competitive intelligence, or customer-facing answers, WARP demonstrates that the retrieval layer is now an attack surface that requires content provenance validation, not just domain reputation checks.

### Malicious JetBrains and VS Code Extensions Stole AI API Keys from 70,000 Developers Over Eight Months

**A coordinated malware campaign on the JetBrains Marketplace deployed at least 15 fake IDE plugins that quietly stole AI provider API keys — including OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepSeek credentials — from thousands of developers over an eight-month campaign running from October 2025 to June 2026.** The plugins posed as helpful AI coding assistants but hid credential-theft routines beneath their surface, accumulating close to 70,000 combined installs across seven vendor accounts. ([Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/malicious-jetbrains-and-vs-code-extensions-steal-api-keys/) | [Aikido Security](https://www.aikido.dev/blog/multiple-jetbrains-ide-plugins-caught-stealing-ai-keys) | [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/malicious-jetbrains-marketplace-plugins-steal-ai-api-keys-from-developers/))

**The campaign.** Researchers at Aikido Security identified and disclosed the campaign. The 15 plugins targeted DeepSeek, OpenAI, and SiliconFlow API keys specifically — the credentials that give access to expensive AI inference infrastructure. The Cloud Security Alliance noted in a related report that IDE plugin ecosystems have become a primary attack surface for AI credential theft, and that supply chain integrity controls have not been extended to these environments.

**The broader pattern.** Alongside the JetBrains campaign, researchers tracked two related threats active during the same window: the GlassWorm worm targeted the Visual Studio Code Marketplace and the OpenVSX Registry, while a separate Nx Console supply chain compromise hit GitHub's internal repository. Together, they reflect a wider pattern of attackers converging on developer tools as a high-value entry point for AI credential theft.

**Why it matters:** AI API keys are the new crown jewels. A single stolen OpenAI or Anthropic API key can be used to run inference at the victim's expense — denial-of-wallet attacks have been observed burning $46,000 per day. The developer toolchain — IDE plugins, VS Code extensions, npm packages — is now a well-recognized and actively exploited target for AI credential harvesting, and marketplace integrity controls have not kept pace.

### F5 Patches Two Critical NGINX Open Source RCE Flaws — HTTP/3 and HTTP/2 Both Affected

**F5 has released security updates addressing two critical remote code execution vulnerabilities in NGINX Open Source — CVE-2026-42530 (CVSS 9.2) in the HTTP/3 QUIC module and CVE-2026-42055 (CVSS 9.2) in the HTTP/2 proxy module — affecting the most widely deployed web server and reverse proxy on the internet.** Both require ASLR bypass for full exploitation, and F5 has not confirmed in-the-wild exploitation. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/f5-patches-two-critical-nginx-open.html))

**The vulnerabilities.** CVE-2026-42530 is a use-after-free in the `ngx_http_v3_module` triggered by a specially crafted HTTP/3 session reopening a QPACK encoder stream. CVE-2026-42055 is a heap-based buffer overflow in the `ngx_http_proxy_v2_module` and `ngx_http_grpc_module` modules triggered when proxying HTTP/2 traffic with specific configuration combinations. Affected versions span NGINX Open Source 1.30.0–1.31.1, NGINX Plus R33–R37, NGINX Ingress Controller 3.5.0–5.5.0, and NGINX Gateway Fabric 1.3.0–2.6.3.

**The mitigations.** For CVE-2026-42530, disable HTTP/3. For CVE-2026-42055, remove the `ignore_invalid_headers off` directive or reduce `large_client_header_buffers` below 2 MB. Patched versions: NGINX Open Source 1.31.2 and 1.30.3.

**Why it matters:** NGINX powers an estimated 30%+ of all websites and is the most common reverse proxy for AI inference endpoints, API gateways, and microservice architectures. Critical RCE flaws in both HTTP/3 and HTTP/2 modules mean that any organization running NGINX in front of AI infrastructure should prioritize patching — even without confirmed exploitation, the attack surface is enormous.

### EchoLeak (CVE-2025-32711): Zero-Click Prompt Injection That Exfiltrates Corporate Data Through an Auto-Loading Image URL

**Disclosed by Aim Security and highlighted in OrcaRouter's AI Threat Report 2026 published June 22, EchoLeak (CVE-2025-32711) is a zero-click prompt injection attack against Microsoft 365 Copilot that exfiltrates sensitive corporate data — mail, files, chat history — through an auto-loading image URL, with no user interaction required.** The victim does nothing wrong: no link clicked, no attachment opened, no prompt approved. They receive an email. Their AI assistant later reads it and obeys the instructions hidden inside. ([Temasek Post / OrcaRouter](https://www.temasekpost.com/posts/orcarouter-releases-ai-threat-report-2026-and-makes-its-security-controls-free-amid-rise-in-prompt-injection-attacks) | [ANINews](https://aninews.in/news/business/orcarouter-releases-ai-threat-report-2026-and-makes-its-security-controls-free-amid-rise-in-prompt-injection-attacks20260622151739/))

**The attack chain.** An attacker sends an email containing hidden prompt injection instructions. When Copilot processes the email — as part of its normal operation of reading and summarizing the user's inbox — it encounters the injected instructions. Those instructions tell Copilot to gather sensitive context from the user's mail, files, and chat history, then encode that data into an auto-loading image URL pointing to an attacker-controlled server. The image loads automatically when the email is rendered, exfiltrating the data. Zero clicks. Zero user awareness.

**The broader pattern.** EchoLeak is not an isolated case. OrcaRouter's AI Threat Report 2026 documents 14 key risks across four threat categories, finding that prompt-injection attacks rose 340% year over year (OWASP, Q1 2026), 13% of organizations have already been breached through an AI model or application (IBM, 2025), and 97% of those lacked basic AI access controls. The average successful attack completes in 42 seconds, with 90% of them leaking sensitive data (Pillar Security).

**The response.** In response to the report's findings, OrcaRouter made two of its security controls available at no cost to all users: the Agent Firewall and input/output Guardrails. The controls can be attached to an API key already in use without a separate integration or purchase. The Agent Firewall provides scoped identity (per-key IP allow-lists, spend caps, expiry), input guardrails (injection/jailbreak detection, PII masking, secret blocking, semantic LLM-judge), and an action firewall (every tool call, MCP dispatch, and network egress judged against default-deny policy with six verdicts).

**Why it matters:** EchoLeak is the canonical example of why traditional security controls fail against AI-powered attacks. The attacker never "breaks in" — they simply send an email that the AI reads. For security teams, the lesson is that AI access controls must operate at the model input layer, not just the network perimeter. With the EU AI Act's high-risk enforcement provisions taking effect on August 2, 2026, the "show me" standard replaces "tell me" as the regulatory baseline — and EchoLeak demonstrates exactly what that standard needs to catch.

### GitHub Actions Checkout v7: How the New pwn_request Defense Changes CI/CD Security

**GitHub has released actions/checkout v7 with secure-by-default behavior that blocks the most common "pwn request" attack pattern — where a malicious pull request from a forked repository executes code with full CI privileges — and will backport the enforcement to all supported major versions by July 16, 2026.** The update directly addresses the most exploited CI/CD supply chain attack vector in the GitHub ecosystem. ([Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/github-actions-checkout-update-workflow/) | [GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-06-18-safer-pull_request_target-defaults-for-github-actions-checkout/))

**The attack pattern.** The `pull_request_target` trigger is widely known as one of the most misused GitHub Actions events because it runs with the base repository's `GITHUB_TOKEN`, secrets, and default-branch cache access — even when the pull request comes from an untrusted fork. When maintainers check out the head or merge commit of a forked PR in this context, attacker-controlled code runs with full CI privileges. This pattern has driven multiple supply-chain compromises across the ecosystem.

**What v7 changes.** The new version refuses to fetch fork pull request code in `pull_request_target` workflows and in `workflow_run` jobs where the triggering event is a `pull_request*` event. The action fails if the `repository` input resolves to the fork's repository, if `ref` matches `refs/pull/<number>/head` or `refs/pull/<number>/merge`, or if `ref` resolves to the fork PR's head or merge commit SHA. Pipelines pinned to floating tags like `actions/checkout@v4` will automatically inherit the safer defaults on July 16. Pipelines pinned to specific SHAs must be upgraded manually.

**The opt-out.** For legitimate use cases where fork PR code must run with elevated trust — coverage generation using private artifact registries or authenticated checks on incoming changes — maintainers can add `allow-unsafe-pr-checkout` to the step. The input name is intentionally loud and descriptive so its presence is obvious during code review.

**The limitations.** Workflows remain vulnerable if they manually pull and execute untrusted code using `run` blocks that call `git` or the `gh` CLI to fetch arbitrary refs. Unsafe patterns triggered by other events, such as `issue_comment`, also remain out of scope. GitHub may expand hardening to additional event types in the future.

**Why it matters:** For organizations running AI development workflows on GitHub — which is the majority of the AI ecosystem — the `pull_request_target` pwn request pattern is the most common CI/CD supply chain attack vector. The v7 update eliminates the most prevalent variant without requiring workflow authors to redesign their pipelines. The July 16 backport deadline means every organization has three weeks to audit their workflows and confirm they are not relying on the now-blocked pattern.
