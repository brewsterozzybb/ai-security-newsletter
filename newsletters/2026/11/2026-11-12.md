## 1) wfx-loot: stolen GitHub tokens push a CI stealer that grabs AI-provider keys (incident)
**What changed:** Aikido (page dated Nov 12, no time on the page; RSS Wed 8:45 PM EST) says that from about 12:45 UTC on November 10, **4,837** compromised accounts opened probe pull requests in **61,764** repositories. In the same post it reports that the payload ran and credentials were exfiltrated in at least **1,835** repositories, and separately that it has indications of runs in at least **1,806** (a figure it says may understate impact, because the actor deletes workflow logs). The payload collects Actions secrets and credentials for OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, Hugging Face, Cursor, Copilot, and other AI tools. StepSecurity (RSS Thu 5:47 AM EST) says GitHub search showed more than **63,000** probe PRs as of 07:40 UTC November 12; that exfil switched to Telegram (first seen 01:10 UTC Nov 12; Telegram-only from about 05:30 UTC); and that **173** probe PRs from **19** accounts were made through the Fly.io GitHub App, with "no evidence of a breach of Fly.io's own infrastructure." "The attacker does not exploit a bug in GitHub Actions."
**Limits:** Every count belongs to the named vendor. Aikido's 1,835 and 1,806 figures both appear in the same post. The initial token-theft vector is unknown. The campaign began in the prior window; the first primaries are in-window.
**Action:** Hunt for `wfx-docs` branches and "docs: dependency audit notes" PRs; rotate CI, cloud, and AI-provider secrets reachable from affected workflows. Blocking published IPs is not enough after the Telegram switch.

## 2) SCKit worm via ScreenConnect: 288 endpoints at 48 orgs (incident; MSP)
**What changed:** Huntress (published Nov 11; update Wed 11:34 PM EST) says rogue ScreenConnect clients reached **at least 288** managed endpoints across **48** organizations in about **90 minutes**, including current builds 26.6.6.9747 and 26.7.6.9783, and delivered SCKit. Huntress's assessment is that the activity "seems to be related to" **RMM+ 1.0.31 and 1.0.32**, which would accept a blank access key if one was never set, bypassing ScreenConnect accounts, roles, and MFA. ConnectWise (advisory Wed 5:45 PM EST) says it has "found no evidence of a compromise of ConnectWise infrastructure or a vulnerability in ScreenConnect itself," identified about **400** partner environments with an RMM+-family extension, disabled five such extensions where it can, and told on-premises partners to disable them by hand. Huntress: do not triage infected hosts over ScreenConnect.
**Limits:** 288 / 48 / 90 minutes are Huntress's. The blank-key root cause is Huntress's assessment; ConnectWise has not confirmed it. No CVE. Huntress was read from a saved page copy.
**Action:** Disable the listed extensions, revoke sessions, and investigate from a channel other than ScreenConnect.

## 3) N-able: 4EverAble2Shell and Agent2Shell work on patched HF2 (incident follow-up)
**What changed:** A material follow-up to the Nov 10/11 Able2Shell and HF2 items, not a new CVE main. The Hacker News, citing the researcher, says PoCs work against N-central **2026.4 Hotfix 2** (build 2026.4.1.26) when the Legacy API is on: unauthenticated root RCE (4EverAble2Shell) and SYSTEM on Windows agents if the appliance ID is known (Agent2Shell). N-able, quoted by THN (Thu 6:51 AM EST), says the server issue "depends on a non-default configuration in which the Legacy API is turned on and exposed to the public internet," that it is off by default, that customers should disable it, and that hosted environments are not affected. Previdian (Thu 1:12 AM EST) recorded **4** exploitation attempts from **1** source IP and does not claim a completed customer compromise. THN, "as of writing," said "there is no evidence of successful exploitation." huntback (dated Nov 12; CMS published_time 09:00 UTC / 4:00 AM EST) says that at **12:50 UTC** a mining pool listed **21** live hosts serving the N-central login page, **19** of them on build 2026.4.1.26.
**Limits:** huntback's 21 / 19 count is one vendor's and is not independently confirmed here. Do not treat it as resolving THN's earlier "no evidence of successful exploitation" line. N-able's new advisory was not read from the vendor page.
**Action:** Disable Legacy API on on-premises N-central; do not assume HF2 is sufficient if that API is exposed.

## 4) Symantec: AI-style scripts revived a stalled U.S. retailer intrusion (vendor IR)
**What changed:** Symantec (Thu 6:00 AM EST) describes a September intrusion at a U.S. retailer in which hand-typed commands were blocked, then the next day came "around **50**" long, error-free PowerShell scripts over roughly two hours, with comments "that read as a model's reasoning." About **10 minutes** after first querying SolarWinds Orion, the attackers used Orion's bundled `erl.exe` and the RabbitMQ `.erlang.cookie` to run commands on a second server. Symantec reads the timing as "more consistent with an operator relaying output to an AI tool than with an autonomous agent," and calls this one of "dozens" of AI-assisted attacks it has investigated since May.
**Limits:** Every inference about AI use is Symantec's. No encryption or data theft was observed, per Symantec.
**Action:** Alert on a block followed by a sudden shift to long, clean scripts. Audit Orion cookie and `erl.exe` paths.
