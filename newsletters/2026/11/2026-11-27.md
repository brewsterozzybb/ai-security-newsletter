## 1) SAP: reported GitHub-token theft and source-code tampering under "Project Granite" (consulting emergency)

**What changed:** Der Spiegel (dated 26.11.2026, 20.14 Uhr, no zone on the page; visible teaser, body paywalled) reports a security incident at SAP under the headline "Project Granite," which Handelsblatt calls SAP’s internal name for its emergency measures, writing: "Womöglich haben die Täter sich von KI-Systemen helfen lassen." In English, that is an unconfirmed suggestion that the attackers may have had help from AI systems.

SAP told SecurityWeek (November 27, 2026, 2:36 AM ET) it had detected suspicious activity, and told The Record (dated November 27th, 2026; no time on the page) it had identified suspicious activity. "Based on our investigation to date, we have found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data."

Handelsblatt (dated 27.11.2026, 00:21 Uhr, no zone on the page), citing company circles, says software development is affected. "So schränkte der Konzern den Zugriff auf Github ein, stoppte automatisierte Abläufe in der Softwareentwicklung und tauschte Signaturschlüssel aus" — SAP restricted GitHub access, stopped automated development workflows, and replaced signing keys. Der Spiegel, as relayed by SecurityWeek, reported that attackers obtained employee authentication tokens and used them to copy and alter source code on GitHub.

**Limits:** SAP has confirmed the incident and the customer-data finding. The tokens, code changes, pipeline stops, signing-key replacement, and the AI-assist claim come from Der Spiegel and Handelsblatt insider reporting and are unconfirmed by SAP. The Der Spiegel body is paywalled. Vector, actor, and whether any proprietary or customer-bound code was changed remain undisclosed.

**Action:** Treat unexpected GitHub token use and signing-key changes as live incident-response issues, and watch SAP guidance if you consume its software or developer artifacts.

## 2) Reuters: U.S. Air Force Cloud One breached; one source says attackers reached the control plane (consulting emergency)

**What changed:** Reuters, carried by HuffPost syndication (Nov 27, 2026, 06:35 AM EST), reports that hackers breached the Air Force’s Cloud One platform. "One source said that the cyber intruders exploited a software vulnerability to reach the control plane." Reuters "could not establish the identity of the hackers, the full scope of the intrusion or whether any data had been stolen." The Air Force said it "does not comment on the security posture, vulnerabilities or operational status of specific networks and systems."

Investigators called in Cyber Command, Reuters reports. Migrations of back-office applications to Cloud One have been paused, updates to applications already on the platform were blocked, and some users had administrator rights revoked, according to people familiar with the matter, who spoke on condition of anonymity. Reuters notes the report comes amid "heightened anxiety over AI-powered intrusions" but does not present evidence that this breach was AI-powered. No CVE or actor is named.

**Limits:** The account is from a single outlet, via HuffPost syndication, and rests on anonymous sources. The Air Force declined to comment on the system.

**Action:** If you operate on or connect to Cloud One, follow official Air Force direction; do not infer a CVE, actor, or AI role from this reporting.
