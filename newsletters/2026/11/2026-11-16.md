## 1) SANS ISC: filter-evading scans on LiteLLM CVE-2026-42271 and Starlette CVE-2026-48710 (honeypot; CVE)

**What changed:** SANS ISC (Mon 8:11 AM EST) says that "this weekend" its honeypots started seeing `POST /mcp-rest/test/tools/list` requests that appear to exploit LiteLLM CVE-2026-42271 chained with Starlette "BadHost" CVE-2026-48710. Both CVEs are already on KEV (added June 8 and Sept 2). The diary, citing a Horizon3 write-up, describes the chain as unauthenticated remote code execution. New in this diary are **odd Host headers** used, SANS says, "maybe to evade some of the filters built around the PoC exploits": `localhost]`, `[localhost`, `127.0.0.1]`, and `litellm.proxy`. SANS notes Starlette versions prior to 1.0.1 do not validate the Host header, and that LiteLLM then exposes an MCP test endpoint that usually requires authentication. "This isn't a new vulnerability, and exploitation has been reported back in July." Johannes Ullrich advises writing filters for the vulnerability, not a specific exploit, and to "allow only specific hostnames." Horizon3 (write-up dated June 1, updated Aug 1) says to upgrade LiteLLM to 1.83.7 or later and Starlette to 1.0.1 or later. The in-window news is the evasion variants, not a first disclosure.

**Limits:** Honeypot scans only; the diary gives no victim or volume figures. SANS does not mention ARTEX.

**Action:** Allow-list expected hostnames (or reject illegal hostname characters), and apply the Horizon3 patch versions if you run a LiteLLM proxy or other Starlette apps.

## 2) Hudson Rock: an infostealer market sells AI-written victim profiles (vendor research; AI-native)

**What changed:** Hudson Rock (Sun 2:02 PM EST) describes a newly advertised marketplace that attaches an AI-generated profile to stolen infostealer logs — owner, workplace, and which accounts are worth buying — and offers plain-language search. A visible query, "citrix access to healthcare provider," returns a **$10** listing titled "The Ophthalmology Clinic Front Desk Dump." Another **$10** listing, "The AI Researcher's Workstation Dump," claims "valid Anthropic Claude API session tokens"; Hudson Rock says that is "the seller's wording" and that the screenshot does not establish the token type or current validity. Hudson Rock matched a distinctive Firefox profile identifier from a listing preview to a record in its own Cavalier database. The forum advertisement is **dated November 14, 2026** (weekend gap); the Hudson Rock article is in-window. The operator **claims** a database of tens of millions of logs, more than one billion parsed accounts, and prices starting at $0.25 — figures Hudson Rock calls "the operator's assertions."

**Limits:** Inventory and prices are seller claims. Hudson Rock notes that a shared computer "can contain traces of several people," and that a visit to an organization's website does not establish employment. Hudson Rock sells the Cavalier and Enki products it cites.

**Action:** Treat natural-language search over stealer logs as a faster path to role-specific initial access, including listings that advertise AI-provider tokens.

## 3) uBlock Origin one-click UXSS, found mostly by GPT-5.6 Sol (disclosure; AI-native)

**What changed:** A GitHub security advisory (Sun 11:27 AM EST, severity High, no CVE) and a researcher write-up (Sun 1:30 PM EST) describe a one-click universal XSS in uBlock Origin. A site could iframe the element picker, leak the per-install extension UUID, and — after a single click — cause the extension to add a **trusted filter** that runs JavaScript on any site. The advisory says versions **1.75.2 and lower** are affected and that the issue is patched in **1.75.4**. The write-up focuses on the Firefox MV2 build and says a similar but "less problematic" issue also affected uBO Lite (MV3). On how it was found, the researcher writes: "GPT-5.6 Sol did most of the hard work of finding the issue." The researcher says they verified the finding, wrote the prose, and that Claude wrote most of the proof-of-concept code.

**Limits:** The attack needs a site the user visits and a click. The "AI found it" account is the researcher's. The advisory does not describe exploitation in the wild.

**Action:** Update uBlock Origin to 1.75.4 or later.
