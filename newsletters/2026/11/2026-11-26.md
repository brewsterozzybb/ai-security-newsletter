## 1) Microsoft: state-linked groups abuse ARTEX; Wiz finds Intent IDs in victim logs (AI-native)

**What changed:** Microsoft Threat Intelligence (dated November 25; no time on the page) writes: "As of November 2026, Microsoft Threat Intelligence has observed both state-sponsored and financially motivated threat actors such as Storm-0940, Storm-3358, Onyx Sleet, Pearl Sleet, Crystal Typhoon, and Salt Typhoon integrating ARTEX into their tooling." Recent campaigns, it says, used ARTEX to search public repositories for exposed secrets, enable supply-chain compromise, and scan internet-facing systems. ARTEX "can advance from reconnaissance to persistent, privileged access to a target organization in a matter of minutes." "ARTEX does not introduce new attack techniques; it uses common, pre-existing penetration testing tools and techniques."

Wiz (dated November 25, 2026; no time on the page) says Intent IDs from ARTEX and Cairn "are leaking into victim logs." ARTEX, it writes, backs intents in "a single BIGSERIAL sequence" and "never resets it by default." "We found 36 Intent IDs on the AWS side, in SSM command comments and resource names, and 34 on the Azure side, in resource names, tags, and a role assignment description. All of them came from one climbing sequence, linking both to the same run even though the identities have nothing in common." Wiz’s caveat: "Intent ID pacing is a corroborating signal, not standalone attribution."

**Limits:** The actor list is Microsoft’s. The 36/34 counts and the attribution caveat are Wiz’s.

**Action:** Hunt for bursty, machine-speed probing and for leaked Intent IDs in logs and resource names, and treat those IDs as a corroborating signal only.

## 2) 1Password: attacker used an employee GitHub account to push malicious backend code (confirmed intrusion)

**What changed:** 1Password (dated November 25, 2026; no time on the page; Hacker News Wednesday 12:18 PM EST, 1,510 points) says: "On November 19, 2026, 1Password began investigating indications that an attacker gained access to an employee’s corporate GitHub account. The actor used the account to access source code and push malicious code to backend services, which downloaded administrative data pertaining to our customers." "At no time were customer vault contents accessed by, or available to, the attacker."

"The attacker downloaded account records for a subset of our users." They include names, email addresses, and "Tokens present in certain emailed links for invitations, provisioning and account recovery." "The attacker also copied source code from private repositories and gained access to our CI/CD system." "We have triggered a rotation of all credentials which may have been exposed." Not affected, it lists, are "Your vaults and the items in them," the keys that protect those vaults, and "Account recovery keys." It "Lengthened the codes used in invitation and recovery emails from six digits to ten characters as a precautionary measure." It does not state how many users are in the subset.

**Limits:** The incident narrative is 1Password’s alone. No affected-user count is given.

**Action:** Treat unexpected invitation or recovery emails with care, and follow 1Password’s guidance if you were notified.

## 3) KEV v2026.11.25 adds 10 listings, led by five Mitel MiCollab AWV flaws (CVE; KEV)

**What changed:** CISA’s catalog **v2026.11.25**, dateReleased Wednesday 2:48 PM EST, lists **1,805** entries — ten new listings, all added Nov 25.

Five are in Mitel MiCollab’s Audio, Web and Video Conferencing component: CVE-2026-133323 (OS command injection), CVE-2026-133303 (XML injection), CVE-2026-133275 (unrestricted file upload), and CVE-2026-133324 (not failing securely), all due **Saturday, Nov 28**; and CVE-2026-133311 (weak authentication), due **Dec 9**.

Accenture APEX Labs (published November 2026; no day or time on the page) [PRE-WINDOW] "assesses that a threat actor exploited the MiCollab vulnerabilities in advisory MISA-2026-0012 before Mitel published the advisory and patch on 16 November," citing "web shell activity before 23:00 UTC on 15 November 2026." The actor "reached root on the MiCollab server within 30 minutes" and authenticated to a domain controller "within 32 minutes." APEX Labs "assesses that AI-assisted tooling drove parts of the intrusion," after the actor’s server "contacted two commercial AI API services at the same second that the actor sent its authentication-bypass request," in a window with "no sampled operator traffic." SecurityWeek (November 26, 2026, 6:35 AM ET) reported the write-up.

The other five adds, all also added Nov 25: **CVE-2026-103406**, HPE Juniper Session Smart Conductor and Session Smart Router, due Nov 28, which in CISA’s wording "contain an OS command injection vulnerability that could allow a remote attacker to execute arbitrary commands as root via a specially crafted salt minion ID"; **CVE-2026-133469**, Vates Xen Orchestra, due Nov 28, an unauthenticated authentication bypass via a crafted token; and three Danfoss AK-SM 800A listings — CVE-2023-25914 (path traversal, due Nov 28), CVE-2023-25915 (improper input validation that CISA says "could allow a remote attacker to run arbitrary commands on the affected system," due Nov 28), and CVE-2025-41450 (improper authentication, due Dec 9).

SecurityWeek (November 26, 2026, 8:04 AM ET) reports that on Tuesday Juniper updated its advisory to flag CVE-2026-103406 as exploited, and quotes Juniper: "At least one instance of malicious exploitation of CVE-2026-103406 has been reported to the Juniper SIRT."

**Limits:** KEV wording quoted above is CISA’s. The 30- and 32-minute figures and the AI-API assessment are Accenture’s, from a pre-window report, and rest on sampled telemetry from one victim.

**Action:** Treat the eight Nov 28 items as due Saturday and the two Dec 9 items as dated then. Hunt Mitel MiCollab for compromise that predates the Nov 16 patch.

## 4) Drupal SA-CORE-2026-019 / CVE-2026-136716: Fail2Install bypass, exploited, not yet on KEV (CVE; exploited)

**What changed:** Drupal’s SA-CORE-2026-019 (dated 2026-November-25; no time on the page) covers CVE-2026-136716, a follow-up to Tuesday’s KEV listing of CVE-2026-132165. "SA-CORE-2026-017 recently fixed several vulnerabilities within the Drupal core installer. However, certain sites in unusual or incomplete install states remain vulnerable to site takeover following that advisory, including production sites." "This is being exploited in the wild. Any site that left its installer remotely accessible following SA-CORE-2026-017 may still have been compromised." Fixes are 11.4.14, 11.3.23, or 10.6.23, and sites must "Run update.php. An update hook must run to fix existing production sites in this state." StepSecurity, counting internet-facing sites with Shodan on November 26, says "That is about 31,500 services on affected branches" for the earlier installer flaw, and calls the figures lower bounds.

**Limits:** Exploitation is Drupal’s statement. The 31,500 figure is StepSecurity’s count of services on branches affected by the earlier installer issue, not a count of confirmed 019 compromises.

**Action:** Upgrade now, run update.php, lock down install.php, and check logs and the user 1 account.
