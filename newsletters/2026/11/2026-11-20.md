## 1) vCenter CVE-2026-123890 exploited in early September, Google says (CVE; exploited)

**What changed:** Broadcom's VMSA-2026-0009 (issue date Nov 19; a Nov 20 revision clarifies VMware Tools versions) assigns **CVE-2026-123890** a CVSS 3 score of **9.8**. It describes an authentication-bypass bug in the VMware Directory Service: "A malicious actor with network access to vCenter may exploit this issue to bypass authentication and gain unauthorized access to the system." Fixes listed are vCenter **9.1.1.0200**, **9.0.2.0200**, and **8.0 U3m**, and VMware Cloud Foundation **5.2.5**. For **7.0**, the matrix says to "Contact Broadcom Support if you have extended support contract." There is no workaround. The advisory thanks Mandiant for the report and does not mention exploitation in the wild.

Google Threat Intelligence Group (dated Nov 19; no time on the page) says the suspected PRC-nexus actor **ARMORY CASTLE** (formerly UNC5221) exploited the bug "in early September 2026 using BONESAW." GTIG describes an unauthenticated SRP-6a session-key disclosure over DCE/RPC that yields a session as `administrator@vsphere.local`, after which BONESAW can exfiltrate the LMDB `data.mdb` store, hijack the SAML STS, and create an SSO administrator. It ties initial access in the campaign it describes to BeyondTrust Remote Support **CVE-2026-102764**, then internal scans including port **2012**. "Mandiant has evidence that ARMORY CASTLE had knowledge of CVE-2026-123890 prior to the disclosure of CVE-2026-59309 on July 29, 2026," GTIG writes, while noting that a recovered BONESAW build timestamp "can be modified prior to compilation." The CVE is **not on KEV**.

**Limits:** Exploitation, attribution, and the July-knowledge claim are GTIG/Mandiant's, not Broadcom's. The 9.8 score and patch matrix are Broadcom's. Early September is an event date.

**Action:** If you run vCenter 8 or 9, apply the listed builds and hunt for unexpected SSO admins and SAML-token abuse. On 7.0 with extended support, contact Broadcom; there is no public 7.0 fix in the advisory.
