## 1) Microsoft Research: editing fewer than 10 dormant neurons can backdoor an agent (lab/research)
**What changed:** Microsoft Research (Tue 3:52 PM EST) describes "Lazarus," a technique it says can backdoor an agent by weight-reprogramming fewer than 10 dormant neurons, with no training. The model "passes standard evals and behaves normally, until a trigger appears" — the example is a clock rolling past midnight into 2027 — then emits a command that runs attacker code with the agent's access. The Security Blog (Tue 6:00 PM EST) and the Research post both say Claude Code with Opus 4.5 "discovered most of this" (Research: "autonomously"). Microsoft "has not observed any threat actors weaponizing this technique," and advises diffing checkpoints against a declared base and treating third-party fine-tunes as untrusted until audited.
**Limits:** Every technical claim above is Microsoft's. Not observed in the wild.
**Action:** Add weight diffs to open-weight intake; keep high-impact tool calls behind human approval and tight egress.

## 2) Railway's full October report: ~$740,000 in unauthorized AI spend (official)
**What changed:** Railway published its full incident report Tuesday (9:00 AM EST; last updated Nov 10, 18:02 UTC / 1:02 PM EST) on an October 20–21 intrusion. The in-window event is the report, not the October break-in, and it is not the Nov 6 Microsoft ARTEX-to-Key Vault story. Railway says a build-isolation flaw let the attacker decrypt variables for at most **1.85% (~100,000)** of workspaces, read the histories of **353** builds, and obtain linked-account tokens for **1,852** users, all of which it says it reset. "Unauthorized spend on the stolen keys was estimated to reach ~$740,000." It quotes Wiz that Railway was "defending against abliterated models and a harness similar to ARTEX," and says the attacker was targeting AI provider credentials.
**Limits:** Those counts are Railway's. The ARTEX-like wording is Railway quoting Wiz.
**Action:** Rotate any AI-provider, cloud, or VCS secrets that lived in Railway variables in mid-October, and file refund claims through Railway.

## 3) Patch Tuesday: exploited Outlook NTLMv2 leak is on KEV, due Nov 24 (KEV; exploited)
**What changed:** Microsoft's November CVRF lists **1,362** CVE entries. **1,093** of those were first published November 10 and carry a Microsoft exploitation assessment, including **63** Critical and **one** marked Exploited:Yes — **CVE-2026-124194**. A crafted message file opened in Outlook can connect to an attacker host without the usual external-content warning and disclose the user's NTLMv2 hash; Microsoft says the Preview Pane is not a vector. CISA added it to KEV in catalog **v2026.11.10** (Tue 2:03 PM EST; 1,779 entries, +6), due **Nov 24**. The CVRF credits **"Benoît Sevens using Claude with Anthropic"** and **"nono."** The same release also ships AI-stack fixes, none marked exploited: Azure OpenAI **CVE-2026-119990** (Critical; Microsoft says it is already fully mitigated, no customer action), MCP Gateway **CVE-2026-109492**, Microsoft 365 Agents SDK **CVE-2026-109485**, and GitHub Copilot for JetBrains **CVE-2026-83953**.
**Limits:** Press totals range from 1,093 to 1,260 depending on the counting method; figures above are from the CVRF. Microsoft has not named an actor.
**Action:** Patch Outlook before Nov 24. Apply the AI-stack updates where those products are in use.

## 4) Barracuda ESG chain now exploited; KEV due Nov 13 (KEV; exploited)
**What changed:** The Nov 6 edition recorded Barracuda's **CVE-2026-116606** (ESG authorization bypass) and **CVE-2026-111560** (command injection, also on other Barracuda products) as fixed and not exploited. The Register quotes a late Monday Barracuda update: "We have identified exploitation activity affecting a small number of organizations." On Tuesday, CISA added both to KEV, due **Nov 13**. SecurityWeek, citing Previdian, reports 17 attempts against CVE-2026-116606 between November 9 and 11.
**Limits:** The vendor advisory page did not yield text here; the quoted line is Barracuda via The Register.
**Action:** Patch ESG and any other affected Barracuda products before Nov 13; treat Admin UI exposure as urgent.

## 5) Cisco BroadWorks CVE-2026-20320 added to KEV, due Nov 24 (KEV)
**What changed:** CISA added Cisco's unauthenticated BroadWorks XXE **CVE-2026-20320** to the same Tuesday KEV drop, due **Nov 24**. Cisco's advisory (first published August 19, 2026) rates it CVSS 7.5, says there are no workarounds, and lists Application Delivery Platform, Application Server, Profile Server, and Xtended Services Platform. The Nov 10 edition treated Operation Snowball — which used this bug as an entry point — as Missed (Nov 8) background. The KEV listing is the new fact.
**Limits:** The KEV entry does not attribute a campaign.
**Action:** Confirm BroadWorks is on a fixed release before Nov 24, especially on internet-facing OCI/XSP.
