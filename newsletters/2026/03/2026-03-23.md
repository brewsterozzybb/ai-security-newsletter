# AI Security Newsletter — March 23, 2026

## Headliners

RSAC 2026 hits its full stride today — and the week's policy, tooling, and threat research is landing simultaneously. The White House dropped a national AI legislative framework, the State Department stood up a Bureau of Emerging Threats, and Microsoft unveiled an end-to-end agentic AI security architecture. Meanwhile, attackers didn't wait: a CVSS 10.0 quest KACE flaw is actively exploited, VoidStealer has cracked Chrome's Application-Bound Encryption without privilege escalation, and DarkSword — the second iOS exploit kit in a month — is proliferating across state and financially motivated actors alike. ## Breaking News & Phase Shifts

**[White House Releases National AI Policy Framework: Preempt State Laws, Enable Industry, Avoid New Federal Regulation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/articles/2026/03/president-donald-j-trump-unveils-national-ai-legislative-framework/)**

On March 20, the Trump administration released its National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence — a four-page legislative recommendation document organized around seven pillars. The framework's core posture: AI governance should be handled at the federal level with "minimally burdensome" standards, and Congress should explicitly preempt conflicting state legislation to avoid "fifty discordant" regulatory regimes. The document does not call for new federal AI regulatory agencies; instead, it leans on existing authorities and industry self-governance, while carving out specific protections for children and intellectual property. Critically, the framework dropped the same day the State Department formally notified Congress of its new Bureau of Emerging Threats (see below), and the same week RSAC 2026 opened — creating a rare simultaneous policy-and-threat-intelligence confluence. **Practitioner guidance:** The preemption posture means the regulatory complexity you've been tracking at the state level (California, Colorado, Texas) is likely to collapse into a single federal standard — begin aligning your AI governance documentation to federal baseline expectations now rather than building state-by-state compliance matrices that may be superseded.

**[State Department Launches Bureau of Emerging Threats to Counter AI Weaponization by Iran, China, Russia, North Korea](https://abcnews.com/Politics/state-department-launches-effort-counter-cyberattacks-ai-risks/story?id=131265350)**

The U.S. State Department formally launched its Bureau of Emerging Threats (BET) on March 23, 2026 — a new entity dedicated to anticipating and responding to adversarial weaponization of AI, cyberattacks, space threats, and quantum risks. The bureau comprises five divisions: Cybersecurity, Critical Infrastructure Security, Disruptive Technology, Space Security, and Threat Assessment. Secretary Rubio announced BET nearly a year ago; formal activation now comes as CrowdStrike documents a surge in pro-Iranian threat actor activity following U.S.-Israeli military action in February, and CISA is still investigating the Stryker breach. The BET's explicit Iran, China, Russia, North Korea focus — combined with the simultaneous White House AI framework — signals that AI security is now a standing foreign policy priority, not just a domestic technology issue. **Practitioner guidance:** BET's Disruptive Technology office will produce advisories on adversarial AI use — treat these as high-signal intelligence inputs on par with CISA KEV entries for AI-adjacent threat modeling.

**[Super Micro Computer Co-Founder Charged With Smuggling $2.5 Billion in Nvidia AI Chips to China](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-charges-three-people-with-conspiring-divert-ai-tech-china-2026-03-19/)**

The Justice Department charged Yih-Shyan Liaw (co-founder of Super Micro Computer), Ruei-Tsang Chang, and Ting-Wei Sun with conspiring to smuggle approximately $2.5 billion in Supermicro servers containing Nvidia AI chips to China via Taiwan intermediaries, in violation of U.S. export controls. The indictment, unsealed March 19 in Manhattan federal court, alleges a systematic scheme to evade export license requirements that exist specifically to protect U.S. national security and competitive AI advantage. Supermicro shares fell 33% on the news. The national security framing is direct: these controls restrict AI compute from adversaries; the alleged scheme effectively delivered adversary-grade AI infrastructure to China at a scale that could meaningfully accelerate state-directed AI programs. **Practitioner guidance:** If your AI infrastructure supply chain includes Supermicro hardware procured through third-party resellers, this case is a signal to validate provenance and chain-of-custody documentation — the indictment pattern mirrors classic diversion schemes that security teams should be flagging in procurement risk reviews.

**[Microsoft Unveils Agent 365 and End-to-End Agentic AI Security Strategy at RSAC 2026: Defender, Entra, Purview, Sentinel MCP](https://siliconangle.com/2026/03/22/microsoft-outlines-agentic-ai-security-strategy-new-defender-entra-purview-capabilities/)**

At RSAC 2026, Microsoft detailed a comprehensive agentic AI security architecture spanning agent governance, identity protection, data security, and autonomous SOC capabilities. The centerpiece: Agent 365 — a control plane for AI agents, generally available May 1, providing centralized visibility and governance over deployed agents across the enterprise via Defender, Entra, and Purview integrations. New capabilities include Security Dashboard for AI (now GA), Entra Internet Access Shadow AI Detection (GA March 31), and Sentinel MCP — an MCP server layer inside Microsoft Sentinel that enables AI agents to query and act on security telemetry. Microsoft also cited research showing 80% of Fortune 500 companies are already using active agents. **Practitioner guidance:** Entra Shadow AI Detection is the most actionable immediate announcement — it surfaces unauthorized AI applications at the network layer, directly addressing the shadow MCP and shadow AI problem documented by Qualys and AquilaX this week. Enable it and prioritize reviewing the unmanaged AI usage report before the May 1 Agent 365 GA.

## Technical Deep Dives

**[CVE-2025-32975 (CVSS 10.0): Quest KACE SMA Authentication Bypass Actively Exploited — Mimikatz, RDP Pivots, Payload Drops Observed](https://thehackernews.com/2026/03/hackers-exploit-cve-2025-32975-cvss-100.html)**

Arctic Wolf documented active exploitation of CVE-2025-32975, a maximum-severity authentication bypass in Quest KACE Systems Management Appliance (SMA), beginning the week of March 9, 2026. The flaw allows attackers to impersonate legitimate users without credentials and seize full administrative account control. Observed post-exploitation chain: Base64-encoded payload delivery via curl from 216.126.225[.]156, new admin account creation via runkbot.exe, Mimikatz-based credential harvesting, and RDP pivots to Veeam and Veritas backup infrastructure and domain controllers. The vulnerability was patched by Quest in May 2025 — active exploitation now is targeting unpatched, internet-exposed instances. The KACE SMA is a privileged endpoint management appliance, meaning compromise grants broad lateral movement capability into managed systems. **Practitioner guidance:** Immediately verify KACE SMA versions — fixed releases are 13.0.385, 13.1.81, 13.2.183, 14.0.341, and 14.1.101. Remove SMA management interfaces from internet exposure. The Veeam/Veritas RDP pivot pattern suggests attackers are targeting backup infrastructure for ransomware staging — treat any SMA compromise as a ransomware precursor and isolate backup systems immediately.

**[VoidStealer 2.0: New Infostealer Bypasses Chrome's Application-Bound Encryption Using Hardware Breakpoints — No Privilege Escalation Required](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/voidstealer-malware-steals-chrome-master-key-via-debugger-trick/)**

Gen Digital (Norton, Avast, AVG, Avira) disclosed VoidStealer v2.0, a malware-as-a-service platform that becomes the first infostealer observed in the wild to bypass Google Chrome's Application-Bound Encryption (ABE) using hardware breakpoints — without requiring code injection or privilege escalation. The technique: VoidStealer starts a suspended hidden browser process, attaches as a debugger, sets hardware breakpoints at the moment Chrome's v20_master_key is briefly in plaintext memory during a decryption operation, and reads the key directly. ABE was introduced in Chrome 127 (June 2024) as a protection against exactly this class of attack. VoidStealer has been a MaaS platform since mid-December 2025 with the new bypass in v2.0, active since March 13, 2026. The technique works on both Chrome and Edge (via msedge.dll). **Practitioner guidance:** ABE is no longer a reliable credential theft defense — it has now been bypassed via four distinct techniques in the wild. For high-risk environments (finance, healthcare, executives), supplement browser credential protection with hardware-bound passwordless authentication (FIDO2/passkeys) rather than relying on software-layer browser encryption. Enterprise EDR rules should flag processes that attach to Chrome/Edge as a debugger from unexpected parent processes.

**[DarkSword iOS Exploit Kit: 6 Vulnerabilities, 3 Zero-Days, Full Device Takeover — Second Kit in a Month, Multiple Actors, Global Campaigns](https://thehackernews.com/2026/03/darksword-ios-exploit-kit-uses-6-flaws.html)**

Google Threat Intelligence Group, iVerify, and Lookout jointly disclosed DarkSword — a full-chain iOS exploit kit targeting iPhones on iOS 18.4–18.7 that has been deployed by multiple threat actors since at least November 2025. The kit chains six vulnerabilities: CVE-2025-31277 and CVE-2025-43529 (JavaScriptCore memory corruption), CVE-2026-20700 (dyld PAC bypass, zero-day), CVE-2025-14174 (ANGLE memory corruption), CVE-2025-43510 and CVE-2025-43520 (iOS kernel memory corruption). Three of the six were zero-days at time of DarkSword's first observed use. Confirmed actors include suspected Russian espionage group UNC6353 (targeting Ukraine), and commercial surveillance vendor customers targeting Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Malaysia. DarkSword is the second iOS exploit kit (after Coruna) discovered within a single month, demonstrating active secondary market trading of top-tier iOS exploits among actors of varying sophistication. CISA added three Apple CVEs from this chain to the KEV catalog March 20, with patching required by April 3. **Practitioner guidance:** Update iOS devices to the latest available release immediately — three of the six CVEs in the DarkSword chain are now in CISA KEV. If your organization manages iOS devices with MDM, validate patch compliance reporting. Lookout's "hit-and-run" characterization (full exfiltration within seconds-to-minutes, then cleanup) means detection after the fact is extremely difficult — prevention via patching is the only reliable control.

## Five Things to Take Into the Rest of the Day

1. **The White House AI framework and State Department BET announcement together signal that AI security is now a standing U.S. foreign policy and domestic regulatory priority — and the governance structures are operational, not just proposed.** The preemption posture means state-level AI compliance complexity will consolidate; the BET means adversarial AI weaponization will generate formal State Department advisories alongside CISA KEV entries. For practitioners: start mapping your AI governance program to federal baseline expectations now. The orgs that built state-by-state compliance matrices will need to retrofit them to a federal standard; the orgs that built to a federal baseline will find the transition clean.

2. **VoidStealer's ABE bypass is the fourth Chrome encryption defeat in as many months — the lesson is that software-layer browser encryption cannot be treated as a reliable credential protection control.** ABE was a meaningful advance in June 2024. It has now been bypassed via four distinct in-the-wild techniques, including one that requires no privileges and leaves almost no forensic trace. For any environment where browser-stored credentials protect high-value systems (cloud admin consoles, financial platforms, identity provider dashboards), the transition to hardware-bound FIDO2/passkey authentication is no longer optional. Every week you delay, your credential exposure baseline grows.

3. **CVE-2025-32975 in Quest KACE SMA is a textbook ransomware precursor — the Veeam/Veritas RDP pivot is the tell.** The post-exploitation chain Arctic Wolf documented follows the pre-ransomware deployment playbook exactly: credential harvesting, reconnaissance, pivot to backup infrastructure, lateral movement to domain controllers. The attackers went straight to Veeam and Veritas backup systems, which is where ransomware operators go to disable recovery options before detonating. If you have KACE SMA in your environment and haven't confirmed patching to the fixed versions, treat the system as potentially compromised and audit backup infrastructure access immediately.

4. **DarkSword is the second iOS exploit kit disclosed in a single month, and both have been proliferating across multiple actors of varying sophistication — the mobile exploit market is operating like a mature commodity market.** What was true of Windows exploit kits a decade ago is now true of iOS exploit kits: sophisticated chains get commoditized, distributed to actors beyond their original developer, and deployed in financially motivated campaigns alongside state-sponsored espionage. The "hit-and-run in seconds" exfiltration model means endpoint detection on-device is not your primary defense — patch currency is. Three CVEs from DarkSword are now in CISA KEV with an April 3 deadline. For any org managing iOS endpoints with sensitive data, validate MDM patch compliance today.

5. **Microsoft's Agent 365 and the Entra Shadow AI Detection GA are the most concretely actionable RSAC 2026 announcements for enterprise practitioners — not because Microsoft is the only answer, but because they're the first vendor to GA agentic AI governance tooling that integrates with existing enterprise identity and data stacks.** The shadow MCP and shadow AI problem documented by Qualys, AquilaX, and Varonis this week is real and growing. Entra's network-layer AI application discovery (GA March 31) is a genuine visibility capability for organizations that can't instrument every developer endpoint. The architectural message from Microsoft's RSAC posture — security as a "core primitive" woven into the AI stack, not bolted on — is the correct framing. The implementation is imperfect and vendor-locked, but it ships.
