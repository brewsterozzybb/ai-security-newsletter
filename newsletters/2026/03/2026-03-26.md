## Breaking News & Phase Shifts

**[Kaspersky: Infostealers Mimicking Claude Code, OpenClaw, and Gemini CLI Target Developers via Malicious Search Ads — Amatera and AMOS Deployed](https://smechannels.com/kaspersky-discovers-infostealers-mimicking-claude-code-openclaw-and-other-ai-developer-tools/)**

Kaspersky Threat Research published findings this week on an active malware campaign targeting developers searching for AI coding tools. When users search for "Claude Code download" or similar terms, sponsored search ads deliver results pointing to Squarespace-hosted fake documentation pages that are visually identical to official installation pages. Following the fake instructions deploys infostealers: Windows systems get Amatera (a MaaS infostealer harvesting credentials, browser sessions, crypto wallets, and config files), while macOS systems receive AMOS. Kaspersky confirmed parallel campaigns mimicking OpenClaw and Doubao using the same registered domain infrastructure. The payload yields source code from active projects, corporate authentication credentials, private API keys, and developer account sessions — a perfect pivot into CI/CD pipelines and production systems. This campaign directly extends the developer-targeting theme from North Korean Contagious Interview (VS Code malicious projects, covered March 25) and TeamPCP's LiteLLM compromise — all three attack the same high-value target population through different vectors. **Practitioner guidance:** Add Claude Code, OpenClaw, and Gemini CLI download pages to your corporate browser policy allowlist (official domains only) and block Squarespace-hosted lookalike pages that serve installation scripts. Implement endpoint detection for the Amatera and AMOS infostealer families — CrowdStrike and Kaspersky both have signatures. Warn your developer population: never copy-paste installation commands from a search result without verifying the domain.

**[CISA Orders Federal Patch: CVE-2026-20131 (CVSS 10.0) — Interlock Ransomware Exploited Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center as Zero-Day Since January](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/cisa-orders-us-government-patch/)**

CISA added CVE-2026-20131 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on March 19 and gave US federal agencies just three days to patch or discontinue use — an unusually aggressive deadline that signals the severity of active exploitation. The flaw, an insecure Java deserialization vulnerability in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center's web-based management interface (CVSS 10.0), allows unauthenticated remote attackers to execute arbitrary code as root. Amazon's MadPot global sensor network found that the Interlock ransomware group had been exploiting this as a zero-day since at least January 26, 2026 — weeks before Cisco patched it on March 4 — giving attackers an asymmetric window during which defenders had no patch to apply. Cisco FMC serves as the "administrative nerve center" for entire enterprise firewall estates; compromise delivers centralized control over firewalls, intrusion prevention, URL filtering, and advanced malware protection across all managed devices. The Interlock group discovered their operational toolkit via a misconfigured infrastructure server, allowing Amazon to map their full multi-stage attack chain. **Practitioner guidance:** If you run Cisco FMC and haven't patched, do it today — don't wait for a change window. Isolate the FMC management interface from internet-exposed networks immediately as a compensating control. Audit FMC access logs from January 26 forward for anomalous authentication attempts and unauthorized policy changes. CISA's three-day mandate applies to federal agencies, but the severity warrants treating it the same in any regulated or enterprise environment.

**[FBI and CISA Joint Advisory: Russian State Actors Hijacking Signal and WhatsApp via Social Engineering — Thousands of Accounts Compromised](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/03/fbi-cisa-warn-of-russian-hackers-hijacking-signal-and-whatsapp-accounts)**

The FBI and CISA issued a Public Service Announcement (PSA260320) warning of an active, widespread Russian-linked campaign targeting Signal and WhatsApp accounts belonging to current and former US government officials, military personnel, political figures, and journalists. The attack technique doesn't break end-to-end encryption — it walks around it by socially engineering targets into adding attacker-controlled devices as linked devices, posing as "Signal Support" or "Signal Security Bot." Thousands of accounts are confirmed compromised. European intelligence services AIVD and MIVD issued parallel warnings in the same window, indicating a coordinated, multi-theater campaign. The technique scales horizontally: once the playbook is public, criminal actors copy it for financial fraud and account theft. Current targets are high-profile, but the identical social engineering technique applies to any employee with access to sensitive enterprise communications running on consumer messaging apps. **Practitioner guidance:** Immediately audit linked devices on Signal and WhatsApp accounts used for sensitive communications — remove any unrecognized sessions. Enable Signal's Registration Lock feature to prevent SIM-swap-based registration attacks. For enterprise communications, the use of consumer messaging apps for any sensitive content should be treated as out-of-policy given this threat landscape. Do not accept "device linking" requests from anyone via a messaging app — Signal support does not contact users through the app.

**[CrowdStrike Announces AIDR Expansion at RSA 2026: "Living Off the AI Land" (LOTAIL) Attack Class Detected in Desktop AI Agents, Shadow AI Governed via Falcon](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/new-crowdstrike-innovations-secure-ai-agents-govern-shadow-ai/)**

CrowdStrike introduced a new attack class at RSA 2026 called "Living Off the AI Land" (LOTAIL) — the exploitation of AI agents' high system permissions and autonomous action capabilities to perform attacker objectives that are indistinguishable from legitimate user behavior. LOTAIL is specifically effective against desktop AI agents like coding assistants and personal AI tools that can execute terminal commands, browse the web, interact with files, and take autonomous actions with developer-level permissions. CrowdStrike is expanding its Falcon AIDR (AI Detection and Response) platform to monitor desktop AI applications at the endpoint level, extending beyond browser-based AI monitoring. Additionally, the company is launching shadow AI discovery and governance capabilities across endpoints, SaaS environments, and cloud — specifically targeting unregistered agents and AI tools deployed without security review. The LOTAIL framing is the most precise articulation of the agentic insider threat vector: the agent isn't compromised, it's being given legitimate-looking instructions that happen to serve the attacker. **Practitioner guidance:** Deploy AIDR or equivalent endpoint monitoring for AI desktop applications now — standard EDR doesn't instrument the agent action layer. Catalog every AI agent with access to developer machines in your environment; unknown agents should be treated as shadow AI and flagged for governance review. The LOTAIL attack class requires behavioral baseline monitoring, not signature detection — the agent behavior is technically legitimate.

## Technical Deep Dives

**[OWASP GenAI Security Project Releases Top 10 for Agentic AI Applications 2026 + Secure MCP Server Development Guide](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/owasp-genai-security-project-expands-ai-security-frameworks-ahead-of-rsa-2026-celebrates-continued-sponsor-support-302718289.html)**

OWASP's GenAI Security Project released two major frameworks ahead of RSA 2026: the Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 (a globally peer-reviewed framework identifying the most critical security risks facing autonomous AI systems) and a dedicated Guide for Secure MCP Server Development. The frameworks reflect OWASP's 25,000+ member community and are rapidly becoming the de facto standard reference for AI security practitioners and policymakers. The release context is notable: multiple OWASP GenAI Project sponsors have been acquired in the past year — SPLX by Zscaler, Pangea by CrowdStrike, Calypso AI by F5, Lakera by Check Point, and Prompt Security by SentinelOne — signaling that OWASP's open taxonomy is actively shaping commercial acquisitions. The updated Landscape Guide expands coverage of the full LLM and GenAI lifecycle: development, testing, deployment, and governance. The Agentic Top 10 includes MCP server exploitation, tool chain attacks, and data exfiltration via agent action — attack classes confirmed in real-world incidents this month. **Practitioner guidance:** Download the OWASP Agentic AI Top 10 at genai.owasp.org and use it as your AI security baseline evaluation rubric. The Secure MCP Server Development guide is directly applicable to any team building or deploying MCP servers — cross-reference every public MCP server in your environment against it. OWASP frameworks becoming acquisition targets means the open-source baseline is now commercial-grade; there's no excuse for not using it.

**[Elastic Security Labs: VoidLink Rootkit — AI-Assisted Malware Framework Written in Zig, 88,000 Lines in Under a Week, LKM+eBPF Hybrid Stealth](https://www.elastic.co/security-labs/illuminating-voidlink)**

Elastic Security Labs published a deep technical analysis of VoidLink, the cloud-native Linux malware framework first documented by Check Point Research in January 2026. Elastic obtained source code from the rootkit's development history and found a multigenerational framework — four major iterations spanning CentOS 7 through Ubuntu 22.04 — with the latest "Ultimate Stealth v5" adding delayed hook installation, anti-debugging timers, and XOR-obfuscated module names. The rootkit uses a hybrid LKM+eBPF design (rare in the wild): the LKM handles deep kernel manipulation and ICMP-based covert command channels, while eBPF programs handle userland visibility manipulation. Check Point's earlier analysis established that the entire 88,000-line framework was written by a single Chinese-speaking threat actor using the TRAE AI IDE in under a week — roughly 30 weeks of traditional development effort compressed by AI assistance. Simplified Chinese comments and Alibaba Cloud C2 infrastructure confirm attribution. The code contains tutorial-style annotations consistent with multi-turn AI conversations, but no forensically unique AI artifact exists in the compiled binary — the output is functionally indistinguishable from human-authored code. **Practitioner guidance:** AI-assisted malware development eliminates the former "complexity barrier" for sophisticated kernel-level implants. Behavioral and kernel-level detections (eBPF-based EDR hooks, integrity checking of kernel module loads) are now required — signature AV is insufficient for structurally novel malware written from scratch in under a week. Audit loaded kernel modules and eBPF programs in your Linux environments for unexpected entries; VoidLink masquerades as `vl_stealth` or `amd_mem_encrypt`.

**[Mandiant M-Trends 2026: Initial Access Handoff Collapses to 22 Seconds — Voice Phishing Now #2 Initial Vector, Exploits Still #1](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/03/24/mandiant-m-trends-2026-report/)**

Mandiant's M-Trends 2026 report, drawing on 500,000+ hours of incident response in 2025, reveals that the median time between initial compromise and access handoff to a secondary threat group has collapsed to 22 seconds — down from hours in prior years. The division-of-labor model (one cluster gains access, immediately sells or transfers it) has effectively eliminated the window defenders had to detect initial intrusion before follow-on operations begin. Exploits remained the #1 initial vector for the sixth consecutive year (32%), while voice phishing surged to #2 at 11% — active voice social engineering campaigns targeting help desk staff (password resets, MFA changes) drove this shift. Email phishing continues its sustained decline. The 22-second handoff is driven by automated access-broker infrastructure that pre-stages buyer connections; once initial access lands, the clock is already running on a second operation that your detection tools were never designed to catch in this timeframe. **Practitioner guidance:** A 22-second handoff means initial access detection must be real-time with automated blocking — alert-and-investigate workflows are structurally too slow. Implement automated response triggers on first-sign-of-compromise indicators (new user agent on VPN, unusual geolocation, first-ever admin tool execution) that quarantine without waiting for analyst review. For voice phishing: verify every help desk request for credential resets or MFA changes through an out-of-band callback to a pre-registered number — not to a number the caller provides.

## Five Things to Take Into the Rest of the Day

1. **The Kaspersky AI tool infostealer campaign is the broadest-surface developer targeting operation we've seen, and it hits hardest in organizations where developers have high-value credentials with minimal endpoint security.** Amatera and AMOS both specifically target credential stores that developers accumulate over years: GitHub tokens, AWS keys, SSH keys, PyPI publish credentials, Docker registry auth. A single compromised developer machine in a CI/CD-connected environment is a supply chain attack waiting to trigger — exactly the Trivy/LiteLLM cascade pattern. The defense is layered: block lookalike domains, harden credential storage away from plaintext files and environment variables, and deploy endpoint detection for known infostealer families on every developer machine. If you have 50 developers and no endpoint detection on macOS (where AMOS lands), you have a gap that's being actively targeted today.

2. **CVE-2026-20131's exploitation timeline — zero-day since January 26, patched March 4, CISA KEV March 19, your patch deadline March 22 — is the most precise illustration yet of how compressed the defender window has become.** Interlock had 60+ days of uncontested access to any Cisco FMC that faced the internet during that window. The median time-to-patch for critical enterprise network infrastructure is still measured in weeks. That math doesn't work anymore. If your patch SLA for CVSS 10 network infrastructure vulnerabilities is longer than 48 hours for internet-facing management interfaces, it's out of date. The 3-day CISA KEV deadline isn't government-specific — it's the operational standard the threat landscape now requires.

3. **The CrowdStrike LOTAIL ("Living Off the AI Land") attack class deserves a place in your threat model today, not when it becomes more common.** LOTAIL works because AI agents on developer machines have terminal access, file system access, and broad API permissions — the same access profile as a fully-compromised insider threat. The difference is that an AI agent executing LOTAIL commands does so in a way that looks identical to legitimate agent behavior in standard EDR telemetry. Detection requires understanding the baseline of what your AI agents *normally do* so you can identify deviation. You cannot baseline what you haven't inventoried — start the AI agent inventory now.

4. **OWASP releasing the Agentic AI Top 10 at RSA 2026 is the industry's signal that AI security has its own structured vulnerability taxonomy — and that vulnerability taxonomy is now being taught to attackers and defenders simultaneously.** When OWASP published the original Top 10 for web application security, it had a dual effect: it gave defenders a checklist and it gave attackers a roadmap. The Agentic AI Top 10 will have the same dual effect. The organizations that move first to evaluate their agentic systems against this taxonomy — before attackers have fully operationalized it — have a meaningful first-mover advantage. Download it, run it against your production agents, and close the gaps it identifies before your next penetration test finds them instead.

5. **The ARC-AGI-3 benchmark's 0% score for all frontier models is a calibration signal for anyone buying or building AI security tools that claim "autonomous novel threat detection."** Frontier models can recall security procedures, match known attack patterns, and generate plausible analyses of documented techniques. They cannot yet reliably reason through novel attack chains they've never seen — which is precisely what "autonomous detection of unknown threats" requires. This doesn't mean AI security tools aren't valuable; they are. It means the value is in augmenting human analyst speed and coverage, not in replacing human judgment on novel threats. Evaluate AI security tools honestly: what are they actually doing (pattern matching, correlation, summarization) versus what the marketing implies (autonomous reasoning under novelty)?
